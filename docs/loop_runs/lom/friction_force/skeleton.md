## 1. Atomic claim
This concept teaches that static friction is not a fixed value — it self-adjusts to exactly cancel the applied force up to a ceiling f_max = μₛN, past which the body breaks free and friction DROPS to the smaller constant kinetic value μₖN — and only that; it does NOT cover the incline decomposition or the tan θ = μₛ break-away angle (owned by shipped `block_on_incline`), friction driving motion / relative-motion direction subtleties (T12 A26/A7 full — deferred, engine-out), or stacked-block / belt / accelerating-frame contexts (A19/A24/A20 — V2, engine-out).

## 9. Real-world anchor (Rule 35 — universal, culture-neutral, plain English) + curriculum-flex block (Rule 38)
**Primary: pushing a heavy storage box across a floor.** You lean into it — nothing. Push harder — still nothing, the floor answers you newton for newton. Then at one exact effort the box suddenly lurches free, and keeping it sliding feels easier than starting it ever did. That lurch IS μₛ > μₖ, and the whole arc is S1→S4 frame-for-frame. **Secondary: anti-lock brakes** — a car stops best when the tyres do NOT skid, because rolling contact keeps the larger static grip while a skidding tyre gets only the smaller kinetic value (A6's own catalog callout; a car brake is a widest-syllabus-overlap device per 38f). Both findable anywhere on Earth, age-appropriate, physics-true at depth. No places, brands, currency, or festivals anywhere in captions, labels, or narration.
