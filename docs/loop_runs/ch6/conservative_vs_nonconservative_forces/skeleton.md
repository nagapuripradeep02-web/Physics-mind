## 1. Atomic claim

This concept teaches ONE idea: **a force is CONSERVATIVE when the total work it does around any closed path is exactly zero — equivalently, its work depends only on the endpoints, not on the path taken — and friction is NOT: because friction always points against the motion, its work is negative on the way out AND on the way back, so a round trip never cancels it.** Gravity is the conservative exemplar (fixed direction → the return leg's work is equal in size and opposite in sign); friction is the non-conservative exemplar (direction flips with the motion → both legs subtract). It does NOT define potential energy (that is #6 `potential_energy_definition`, which builds ON this property — exactly ONE closing sentence in S4 names it as the next concept, per the approved boundary), does NOT do energy conservation (#9), does NOT account for where friction's negative work goes (#10), and does NOT re-teach the work definition (#1), the sign taxonomy (#2) or kinetic energy (#3) — all prerequisites. The spring is deliberately absent: `'spring'` is NOT a member of the `work_accumulators` closed enum (the surgeon made per-step spring-work accumulation unrepresentable; the honest spring quantity is the state function Uₛ, owned by #8), so "the spring is also conservative" is ceded to #8 and never claimed here on an instrument that cannot show it.
