## 1. Atomic claim + tier

**This concept teaches how a reaction rate is MEASURED — rate = −Δ[R]/Δt = +Δ[P]/Δt, average rate over an interval, instantaneous rate as the settled slope of a shrinking window, the units, and why the measured rate falls as reactant is used up — and only that.** It does not cover WHY temperature/catalyst/concentration change the rate (shipped: `collision_theory_activation_energy`), does not cover how rate DEPENDS on concentration or order (deferred to `rate_law_and_order`), and does not re-teach the speed distribution (`kinetic_particle_theory`).

**Tier: ⭐ (re-argued on this concept's own content — the prior 💎 claim inherited C5 §6 P1 slot #3, which describes a different concept whose content now lives in the shipped `collision_theory_activation_energy` and the sibling; that inheritance was an error and is withdrawn).** Honest whiteboard test: the concentration–time curve, the definition, the units, and the sign convention are content a good teacher does better on a board (design-gate audit, accepted). What the board cannot do — this concept's own irreplaceable core — is (a) **S4: the same measuring tool applied at two places on a curve the sim itself measured**, so the two different numbers are found, not asserted; (b) **S7 (and now S4/S5 via live window sliders): a measuring window the teacher drags along a live-measured curve**, so "what's the rate at 20 seconds?" is answered by moving the window there; (c) **S5: the window visibly shrinking while the number holds steady** — the limit made watchable. This revision makes those capabilities load-bearing across S3–S7 rather than parked in two states. That is a strong ⭐; it may grade up at Checkpoint B if the window-drag experience lands as designed, but the tier is not claimed in advance.

**Graph legibility, answered concretely (the §1 survival condition from REV 1):** there is no right-hand pane — `layout:'with_graph'` no longer reserves one (renderer fact, §10-A). ## 9. Real-world anchor (Rule 35 / 38f — universal, culture-neutral)

**Primary:** an effervescent tablet dropped in a glass of water fizzes hard at first, then weaker, then stops. The bubble stream is a rate meter you can watch: lots of tablet left → fast fizzing; little left → slow; none → zero. Nobody times the whole fizz and calls that "the rate" — the fizzing at the first second and at the last second are plainly different speeds, which is exactly average vs instantaneous rate. **Secondary:** a glow stick is brightest just after snapping and dims over the hours — light output tracks a chemical reaction slowing as its reactants are consumed. Both device-universal, physics-true (bubble/photon output ∝ reaction rate), age-appropriate, plain English. Why it hooks: every Class-12 student has watched a fizz die down; this concept gives that dying-down a number and a slope.
