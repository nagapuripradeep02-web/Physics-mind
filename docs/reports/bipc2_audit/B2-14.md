## Teacher-gate questions
- **`identify_nucleic_acid` s2 calls the A=U / G=C equality "Chargaff's rule".** Chargaff's rule is an
  empirical rule for double-stranded DNA (A=T, G=C); the card extends the name to a uracil-containing
  molecule. Indian guides do this routinely and the sibling card `ts_ipe_b2_mbi_base_percentages` uses
  the name for DNA correctly. Does the Telangana board accept the extended usage, or should the line
  read "the base-pairing equality fails"? Recorded here, not as a finding.
- **`exons_and_introns` s1 defines exons as "the CODING sequences of a gene".** NCERT defines them as
  "the sequences that appear in mature or processed RNA" (the card's second line says exactly this).
  Strictly the UTRs are exonic but non-coding. Both statements are on the card; confirm which the
  board wants first.
- **`dna_polymerase_function` s2 prints "about one error in a billion base pairs".** Standard molecular
  biology (10⁻⁹, machine-checked), but NCERT Ch. 6 states only "high degree of accuracy" and gives the
  ~2000 bp/s rate for *E. coli*, not an error rate. Confirm the figure is acceptable to quote.
- **Every card's own verification note says the mark split is a claim until a Telangana IPE teacher
  confirms it.** The split most worth confirming here is `nucleotide_components`: is the second mark for
  the nucleoside/nucleotide distinction (as MARK_SPLIT says) or for listing the purines and pyrimidines
  (as the insider note implies)? The WRONG row above assumes the card's own split is authoritative.
