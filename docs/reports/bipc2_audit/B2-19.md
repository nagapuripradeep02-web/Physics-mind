## Teacher-gate questions
- **Certification in the release step.** `release_new_variety` s6 prints "Only after this evaluation and CERTIFICATION is the variety released", and `MISTAKE[2]` states the direction as a rule: "Release follows certification, not the other way round." NCERT §9.2.1 step (v) ends at the comparison with the best available local cultivar and never mentions certification; in Indian practice a variety is identified, released and notified first, and **seed** certification of seed lots follows. Does the Telangana scheme expect "certification" in this answer, and in which order? (Recorded here, not as a finding — the guide-book convention may be what the board rewards.)
- **Golden rice as a biofortification example.** NCERT lists Atlas 66 wheat, the high-lysine/tryptophan maize hybrids, iron-fortified rice and the IARI vitamin-enriched vegetables under biofortification (§9.2.4); golden rice appears in Ch. 12 as a genetically modified crop. Is golden rice accepted for a biofortification VSAQ, and is the insider note right to call it half of "the safe pair" rather than, say, the vitamin-A-enriched carrot?
- **How many examples earn the 1 mark** on the biofortification card (two, three or four — see the WEAK row above)?
- **Six-step tissue culture.** Is a five-stage answer (medium preparation and sterilisation merged) accepted, or does the board's scheme expect the six named stages the guide prints?
- **Emasculation.** `release_new_variety` s4 asserts "each cross needs emasculation and hand pollination". True for bisexual flowers; unisexual and dioecious crops need no emasculation. Is the flat statement the expected wording?
