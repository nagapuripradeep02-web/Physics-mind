## Teacher-gate questions
1. **ECG lead count.** `ecg_waves_intervals` s1 prints "Twelve sensors are placed at 12 places and the leads
   are connected to the machine." A standard 12-lead ECG uses TEN electrodes to derive twelve leads, and
   NCERT XI (18.4) describes three leads for a standard ECG with additional chest leads for detail. If the
   Telangana textbook prints "12 sensors at 12 places", leave it; otherwise correct to "ten electrodes give
   the twelve standard leads". Not filed as an error because it may be board wording.
2. **ECG = electrocardiograph or electrocardiogram?** The card prints "ECG stands for electrocardiograph",
   which matches NCERT's own section heading "Electrocardiograph (ECG)". Strictly the trace is an
   electrocardio*gram* and the machine the electrocardio*graph*. Does the board accept either?
3. **"Immortalisation".** `cancer_cell_features` s3 defines it as failure to undergo apoptosis. In current
   cell biology immortalisation is unlimited replicative potential (telomerase) and evasion of apoptosis is
   a separate hallmark. Confirm the board's definition before anyone "corrects" this card.
4. **Apiculture — honey alone or honey and wax?** NCERT XII defines apiculture as maintenance of hives
   "for the production of honey"; this card makes the second mark depend on naming wax as well (see the
   WRONG row on s2).
5. **A whole mark for a conclusion.** `honey_bees_economic` gives 1M to the single line "So honey bees are
   economically important", leaving three marks of content in a 4-mark justify question. Does the board
   score a separate conclusion, or should the fourth mark carry a further product (royal jelly) instead?
6. **"Familial cancer: cancers that are inherited from parents or grandparents."** Strictly the
   predisposition (the mutated gene) is inherited, not the cancer. Confirm the board's wording.
7. **Aquaculture and aquatic plants.** The card's definition names animals only ("prawn, lobster and
   oyster"); aquaculture as defined elsewhere also covers algae and seaweed. Is the animals-only definition
   the board's?
