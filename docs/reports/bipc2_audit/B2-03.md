## Teacher-gate questions
- **Does a membrane get crossed in the apoplast phase?** The card prints "No membrane is crossed and no energy is used" and its s2 MISTAKE[1] forbids saying ions cross membranes there. NCERT Ch.12 says "The passive movement of ions into the apoplast usually occurs through ion-channels, the trans-membrane proteins that function as selective pores." The card follows the standard IPE teaching (apoplast = cell walls and intercellular spaces, so no membrane); NCERT's own sentence names transmembrane proteins. A board teacher must say which wording is safe to print, and whether the prohibition in MISTAKE[1] can stand.
- **"Against the concentration gradient" for the symplast phase.** NCERT states only that entry and exit to and from the symplast require metabolic energy (active). Is "AGAINST the concentration gradient" the wording the board expects, or should it read "can move against the gradient"?
- **Chlorosis for potassium.** NCERT lists N, K, Mg, S, Fe, Mn, Zn and Mo as causing chlorosis, and Ca, Mg, Cu, K as causing necrosis. Will the board accept a bare "chlorosis" as the potassium symptom (which is what the guide printed), or does it want the mottled-chlorosis-plus-marginal-necrosis form the card prints?
- **The reductive-amination equation.** NCERT prints "alpha-ketoglutaric acid + NH4+ + NADPH -> glutamate + H2O + NADP+"; the card adds the free H+, which is the chemically correct form and balances (verified). Is the extra H+ accepted, and is the acid/anion mix in one line (ketoglutaric ACID to glutamATE, which NCERT also prints) accepted either way?
- **Spelling of leg-haemoglobin.** NCERT hyphenates; several Indian texts print "leghaemoglobin" as one word. Both accepted?
- **Step granularity on the root-nodule answer.** The guide prints ten steps, the card twelve, NCERT's paragraph has five sentences. What number of numbered steps does the board expect for four marks?
