## Teacher-gate questions
- **"the enzyme's own end product".** Both `ts_ipe_b2_en_enzyme_inhibitors` s4_feedback and
  `ts_ipe_b2_en_feedback_inhibition` s1_define define feedback inhibition as inhibition by "the enzyme's own
  END PRODUCT". Strictly, the end product of the PATHWAY inhibits an enzyme of that pathway (classically the
  first, committed-step enzyme), so the inhibitor is usually not the immediate product of the enzyme it stops.
  The cards' own boxed line ("The end product switches its own pathway off") is the pathway-level statement.
  Does the board want the simpler enzyme-level wording, or "the end product of the pathway"? Not filed as a
  finding — it is the standard board/guide phrasing.
- **Peroxidase and the H₂O₂ equation.** `ts_ipe_b2_en_types_of_cofactors` s2_prosthetic prints
  "Peroxidase breaks hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen" with `2H2O2 --peroxidase--> 2H2O + O2`. NCERT
  attributes that breakdown to peroxidase *and* catalase together, so the card follows NCERT; in strict
  biochemistry that stoichiometry is catalase's reaction (peroxidase needs a second reducing substrate).
  Confirm the board wants the equation printed under peroxidase.
- **"malonic acid" vs "malonate".** NCERT names the competitive inhibitor "malonate"; the card prints
  "malonic acid resembles the substrate succinate and inhibits succinic dehydrogenase" — a normal Indian-text
  convention, and NCERT itself mixes anion and acid forms ("succinate" / "succinic dehydrogenase"). Confirm
  both are accepted, and likewise "succinate dehydrogenase" for "succinic dehydrogenase".
- **Copper and mercury as non-competitive inhibitors.** `ts_ipe_b2_en_enzyme_inhibitors` s3_noncompetitive
  gives "metal ions of copper and mercury". Some texts class heavy-metal ions as *irreversible* inhibitors
  rather than non-competitive. Confirm the board's classification before the example is changed.
