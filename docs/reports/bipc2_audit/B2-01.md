## Teacher-gate questions
- **Pressure potential sign.** NCERT prints "usually positive, though in plants negative potential or tension in the xylem… is very important"; the Indian guide-book convention is a flat "always positive". A Telangana examiner must rule which the board expects, because it decides the wording of the HARMFUL row above. The mispairing of "always positive" with the *ascent up a stem* is wrong under either convention.
- **The three physical properties for ascent of sap.** NCERT names cohesion, adhesion and **surface tension**; both cards in this group (`ascent_of_sap` s2, `ascent_physical_factors` s2) name cohesion, adhesion and transpiration pull instead. Does the board accept the bank's triad, and is surface tension expected as a named item?
- **Guard-cell inner wall.** NCERT Class 11 prints "The inner wall of each guard cell, towards the pore… is thick and **elastic**"; `stomata_structure_mechanism` s1 writes "THICK and **inelastic**" (the usual guide-book wording) and builds `common_mistakes[0]` on it. Which wording does the board mark?
- **Guard-cell shape.** NCERT says dumb-bell shaped "in grasses"; the card generalises to "in monocots". Is the wider claim accepted?
- **Succulent stomata.** The card explains CAM night-opening as "Organic acids collect at night and make the guard cells turgid" (the acid-metabolism account used in Indian texts). NCERT does not give a guard-cell mechanism for this. Confirm the expected wording.
- **Printed question wording.** `c4_water_efficiency` keeps the source book's "In the wake of transpirational loss, why are C4 plants…", which is an idiom under Rule 41. Keep the board/book question verbatim, or restate it plainly?
