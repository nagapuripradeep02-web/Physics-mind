## Teacher-gate questions
- **Leeches.** Does the board's key put leeches (Hirudinea) with the open type or the closed type? NCERT XI Ch.18 puts annelids in the closed column; some invertebrate-zoology texts describe the leech haemocoelomic system as open. The card currently prints both sides, which is why it is filed HARMFUL — but which single side is correct for this board needs a ruling.
- **Ascidians and echinoderms as open-type examples.** NCERT says "annelids and **chordates** have a closed circulatory system", and ascidians are chordates; zoologically ascidians and echinoderms do have open systems. Which convention does the board mark?
- **Kidney position.** "At the level of the anterior lumbar vertebrae" (card) against NCERT's "between the levels of the last thoracic and third lumbar vertebra" — is "anterior lumbar" the wording the prescribed textbook uses?
- **The two parts of a nephron.** "Bowman's capsule and the renal tubule" (card) against NCERT's "the glomerulus and the renal tubule" against the common "malpighian corpuscle and renal tubule". Which split does the key accept?
- **"Two aortic arches in man."** The card frames the pulmonary trunk and the aorta as the two aortic arches (comparative-anatomy usage); NCERT names them the pulmonary artery and the aorta. Is "aortic arches" / "systemic arch" the expected wording, and is "systemic arch" accepted for the aorta?
- **Vessel nomenclature in figures.** Precaval / post caval vein (heart figure) against superior / inferior vena cava (prose, and the urinary figure), and "dorsal aorta" against "aorta". Which set should the figures standardise on?
- **AVN position.** The card says "near the opening of the coronary sinus"; NCERT says "close to the atrio-ventricular septum". Both are anatomically true — is either preferred?
