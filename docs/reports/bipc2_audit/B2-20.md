  case, not a semi-dwarf variety." Bare "Basmati" is not the NCERT answer, but semi-dwarf basmati
  varieties developed in India do exist (Pusa Basmati 1). Does the TS scheme accept one, or only
  Jaya and Ratna? Left unfiled pending a teacher.
- **Other SCP fungi.** The card gives *Candida utilis* and *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*. Does the
  board also accept *Fusarium venenatum* / *F. graminearum* or *Agaricus campestris*? (Spirulina
  and *Methylophilus methylotrophus* are correctly excluded as non-fungi; note some Indian texts
  still call Spirulina a blue-green alga rather than a cyanobacterium — both readings support the
  card's mistakes line.)
- **Mark split on `ts_ipe_b2_sef_scp_fungi`.** The question asks only for two examples, yet 1 of the
  2 marks is assigned to "What SCP is". Is a bare correct pair of names worth the full 2 marks in
  the TS scheme?
- **Technique name for virus-free plants.** Meristem culture or shoot tip culture — which does the
  board expect, and is either accepted?
- **Line-count convention in `margin_note`.** Across the whole `botany_2` bank the claim "N lines"
  counts the boxed line in 10 steps, excludes it in 14, and matches neither in 26. Within this group
  `scp_fungi.s2_scp` ("Six lines", 6 plain + 1 boxed) and `semi_dwarf_rice.s2_why` ("Six lines",
  5 plain + 1 boxed) each match a different reading, so neither is filed as a defect — but the bank
  needs one rule.
