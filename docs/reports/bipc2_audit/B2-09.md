## Teacher-gate questions
1. **Founders of microbiology.** `ts_ipe_b2_ba_microbiology_definition` prints "Louis Pasteur and Robert Koch are regarded as the founders of microbiology." Several Indian texts name Antonie van Leeuwenhoek as the *Father of Microbiology* and reserve Pasteur/Koch for medical microbiology. Which naming does the Telangana board accept, and does either lose a mark?
2. **Is the founder line part of the second mark at all?** The mark split calls the second mark "What it covers"; the step also prints the founders. A teacher must rule whether the group list alone earns the mark (which decides the `common_mistakes[1]` finding above).
3. **Pleomorphic example.** The card pins *Acetobacter*. Other texts give *Rhizobium*, *Corynebacterium* or *Mycoplasma*. Confirm the board accepts these alternatives, so that no later revision turns "Acetobacter is the safest" into a prohibition.
4. **Genophore vs nucleoid.** Strictly the nucleoid is the *region* and the genophore the *DNA molecule*; the card treats them as synonyms, as most board texts do. Confirm the synonym is accepted.
5. **Dates.** `ts_ipe_b2_ba_transformation` prints Griffith's year (1928); the conjugation and transduction cards print no year (1946 Lederberg & Tatum; 1952 Zinder & Lederberg). Should all three carry the year, or none?
6. **`mark_split` second label on the genophore card** — the printed question is only "What is a genophore?"; a teacher should confirm the comparison with a eukaryotic chromosome is what the second mark is for.
