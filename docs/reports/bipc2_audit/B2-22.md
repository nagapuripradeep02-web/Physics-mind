## Teacher-gate questions
- **`Propionibacterium sharmanii`** (swiss_cheese_holes s2_bacterium) — this is NCERT's printed
  spelling and the card reproduces it exactly; the valid name is *Propionibacterium freudenreichii*
  subsp. *shermanii*. Does the board require, accept, or penalise "shermanii"?
- **Biogas composition on the sewage line** (methanogens s2_do) — NCERT gives two lists: the
  anaerobic sludge digester produces "methane, hydrogen sulphide and carbon dioxide" (sewage
  treatment section), while methanogens on cellulosic material produce "methane along with CO₂ and
  H₂" (biogas section). The card prints the second list on its sewage-sludge line. Both are
  NCERT sentences; which does the board expect for this question?
- **Methanogens are Archaea**, not bacteria. The question itself says "group of bacteria" and NCERT
  calls them bacteria; the card follows NCERT. Confirm no examiner expects the archaeal correction.
- **`Monascus purpureus`** — NCERT calls it a yeast; it is a filamentous fungus, and the card prints
  both. Confirm an examiner will not penalise "fungus" (or the meta-comment, if the WEAK row above
  is not applied).
- **penicillin_scientists `mark_split`** — the question asks only who showed the ROLE (Chain and
  Florey), yet 1 of the 2 marks is allocated to "Who discovered it". The `insider_note` itself says
  "The question asks about the second pair". Does the board award a mark for the Fleming context?
- **lactic_acid_bacteria "the food"** — the card answers MILK (NCERT: LAB "grow in milk and convert
  it to curd"), and names curd in the next line. Does the board expect CURD as the named food?
- **microbes_in_biotechnology "any two"** — the card names only *E. coli* and *A. tumefaciens* and
  nowhere says other pairs are acceptable. Confirm that *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, *Bacillus
  thuringiensis*, *Streptococcus* or *Trichoderma polysporum* also earn full credit, so no future
  prose turns this card's pair into a prohibition.
