## Teacher-gate questions

- **Porins and the name of the diffusion.** NCERT prints the porin sentence inside the *Facilitated
  Diffusion* section. Does the board expect "facilitated diffusion" here, or is "simple diffusion
  through a large open pore" also accepted? (The proposed replacement above avoids the word
  entirely — "passively, down the concentration gradient" — which is safe either way.)
- **"Diffusion = movement of solute."** `osmosis_vs_diffusion` s2 defines diffusion as the movement
  of SOLUTE molecules (the usual two-column convention), while `diffusion_definition_factors` s1 —
  the same unit — defines it, with NCERT, as the movement of molecules down a gradient. Both are
  standard; confirm which wording the board wants in the differentiation answer.
- **Transpiration by day vs at night.** `guttation_vs_transpiration` prints "It occurs during the
  DAY" as a flat contrast, while `transpiration_at_night` teaches that it occurs at night in
  succulents. Confirm whether the guttation comparison should read "mainly during the day".
- **Guttation completeness.** Does the expected 2-mark answer require hydathodes (and root
  pressure) to be named, or are form/time/control enough?
- **Every mark split in this group is unverified** (each `VERIFICATION_NOTE` says so). The cards
  repeatedly assert what "the mark" is — the effect on the rate, the example, all three contrasts.
  A Telangana IPE teacher should rule on whether those halves are really required before the
  prose keeps asserting them.

## Notes

- **Every printed line and boxed answer in the group is biologically sound.** Re-derived and
  confirmed: diffusion is passive and high→low with temperature/gradient/pressure as factors ·
  guard-cell pH RISES on opening (H⁺ pumped out as K⁺ enters) and FALLS on closing · guttation is
  liquid, at night, uncontrolled vs vapour, by day, controlled · pea (proteinaceous) imbibes more
  than wheat (starchy) because proteins imbibe more than carbohydrates · osmosis moves the solvent
  toward the stronger solution across a semipermeable membrane · porins are proteins forming large
  pores in the OUTER membranes of plastids, mitochondria and some bacteria, passing molecules up to
