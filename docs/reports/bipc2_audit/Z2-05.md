## Teacher-gate questions
- **Sacrum and coccyx in a "pelvic girdle" diagram.** Strictly the pelvic girdle is the two coxal
  bones; sacrum + coccyx belong to the vertebral column and make the bony *pelvis*. The card's text is
  careful ("the girdle joins the sacrum"), but `MARK_SPLIT` charges 1M for "Sacrum and coccyx". Does the
  Telangana scheme expect them drawn and labelled? (The card assumes yes; the figure draws both.)
- **Iodopsin as the cone pigment.** NCERT names only rhodopsin and says cones hold three different
  photopigments; iodopsin (photopsin) is the Indian-textbook name the card uses for all three. Confirm
  the board accepts "iodopsin" as the marked word for the cone pigment.
- **"Inverted retina."** The card makes this a marked term ("Leaving out the word inverted"). NCERT
  describes the layer order but does not use the phrase. Confirm it is expected.
- **telodendrites vs telodendria.** The motor-unit card uses "telodendrites" three times; several
  texts write "telodendria" (sing. telodendron), and NCERT says only "terminal branches / synaptic
  knob". Confirm which spelling the board's answer key uses.
- **"mostly acetylcholine"** (synaptic transmission, s4). Acetylcholine is the standard named example
  in the Indian texts; "mostly" is a strong word for a transmitter that is one of many. Confirm the
  board wants acetylcholine named, and whether "may be excitatory or inhibitory" (NCERT's closing
  sentence, absent here) is part of the expected answer.
- **"Synovial capsule" vs "articular capsule"**, and "outer/inner layer" vs "fibrous capsule /
  synovial membrane" — all accepted names; confirm which pair the board's key uses so the text and the
  figure can be made to agree (see the WEAK row above).
