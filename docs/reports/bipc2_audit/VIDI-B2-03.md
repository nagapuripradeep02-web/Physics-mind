The two weak templates fail for different reasons:

- **[why] — the open card is treated as absent.** Six of 24 replies refused to explain the biology of
  the very card in front of them, because the student's phrase "in the plant" was read as naming a
  *different* question. The best replies (`rna_viruses`, `lac_operon_diagram`, `template_vs_coding_strand`,
  `immunosuppressive_agent`) do the right thing — they say the concept is not a plant process, then
  explain the biology anyway. The failures say the same first half and then stop.
- **[remember] — the open step is ignored.** In 15 of 24 the reply drifts off the step that was open
  (almost always s1) into the content of s2. Nothing is *wrong*; the tip is just not for the step the
  student had on screen. Clearest cases: `rna_polymerases` (s1_how_many is "three in a eukaryote, one
  in a bacterium", the reply gives the pol I/II/III product table instead) and `industrial_enzymes`
  (s1_two is the two names, the reply gives only the uses).

## 7. Other observations

- **Markdown leaks as literal asterisks.** One reply is flagged and confirmed:
  `ts_ipe_b2_mbi_griffith_transformation` [telugu] renders `**Transformation**`, `**S-type**`,
  `**R-type**`, `**Streptococcus pneumoniae**`, `**transforming principle**` — five bolded runs a
  student sees as stray asterisks. It is the only markdown leak in 240 replies; the regex flag was
  correct.
- **One reply is duplicated verbatim inside itself.** `ts_ipe_b2_mhw_industrial_enzymes` [why] states
  its whole answer, then immediately restates it with no break — "…and streptokinase dissolves blood
  clots.**The biology is that these enzymes come from microbes, not from the plant itself.** In
  industry, bacteria and fungi are grown in large fermentors…". Note the missing space at the seam,
  which suggests two generations concatenated rather than a stylistic repeat. This is the sole
  OVER_BUDGET flag in the slice (167w/150) and the flag is correct, but the length is a symptom — the
  defect is the duplication. Scored 2.
- **Internal step-ids never leak.** No reply anywhere says "s2_what", "s1_define" or any raw id to the
  student. Steps are referred to as "step 2", "the last step", or by the split's own label ("the 1 mark
  for 'Universal, with a start and stops'"). Clean across all 240.
- **Length is well controlled.** Only one OVER_BUDGET flag in 240 replies, and that one is a
  duplication artefact rather than genuine verbosity. VSAQ (2-mark) cards get noticeably shorter
  replies than SAQ (4-mark) cards, which is the right instinct.
- **Idioms and register.** Mostly plain and literal. Three soft spots worth a note, none scored down:
  `transcription_unit` [explain] runs an extended metaphor ("Think of transcription as reading a page
  out loud… the reading machine sits down… the terminator is the full stop") — vivid, but it is a
  metaphor a second-language reader must decode; `who_proved_dna` [remember] offers a mnemonic that is
  not one ("link **Chase** to the radioactive tracers they used" — nothing in "Chase" points at sulphur
  or phosphorus); `antibiotic_fungi` [why] calls griseofulvin the fungus's "specific weapon in that
  competition", which personifies mildly.
- **The [important] template is the one that handles the STARS/Asked separation well** — 22 of 24
  reported the rank and the exam years as two separate facts, and the eight cards with no Asked line
  were all reported as "the book lists no exam years" rather than "it was never asked", which is
  exactly what those cards instruct. The two that slipped both belong to the STARS defect in §6.
- **Checked and found sound, so noted here rather than as a defect:** `immunosuppressive_agent` names
  *Trichoderma polysporum* as the source of cyclosporin A. That is the NCERT/board answer and the
  historical name in the original literature; the modern name is *Tolypocladium inflatum*. Writing
  anything but *Trichoderma polysporum* would lose the mark, so the card is right to keep it — flagged
  only so a later reader does not "correct" it.
