## Judgement calls

1. **Book vsaq1** (donkey/squirrel escape-velocity scenario) matched to bank vsaq12 — the book poses it as a concrete example, the bank as a generic question; treated as the same underlying physics (escape velocity is mass-independent).
2. **Book saq3** (g vs height h / depth d) matched to bank saq6 — bank derives both variation formulas; the book's explicit "which changes more" comparison was not verified as stated in the card itself.
3. **Book saq16** (T close to the surface) matched to bank saq10 — bank derives T at a general height h; whether the card explicitly takes the h→0 special case was not verified.
4. **Book saq15** (ratio + definitions) marked SECTION-MISMATCH against bank vsaq11 — the full answer is actually assembled from three bank rows (vsaq11 for the ratio, saq1 + saq2 for the two definitions), only one of which needed a section fix; flagged as a composite match rather than one-to-one.
5. **Book laq1** (Newton's law + Cavendish method) marked MISSING rather than section-mismatch — bank vsaq2 arguably covers the "state the law" half, but the Cavendish-method derivation (the harder, larger half of an LAQ) has no bank coverage at all, so classifying the whole entry as MISSING seemed more useful for authoring than a mismatch note.
6. **Book prob13(a)** (numeric escape-velocity calculation) marked MISSING, not MATCHED to bank saq1 — saq1 derives only the symbolic expression with no numeric substitution; since the book explicitly asks to CALCULATE a value and the matching rule requires "same given data for a numerical," a bare formula wasn't counted as satisfying it.
7. **Bank vsaq15** (field-intensity definition, EXTRA) marked "topic present: yes" — g (the same physical quantity) is heavily tested throughout the book, even though the book never uses the term "gravitational field intensity" or asks for its SI unit as a separate concept.
8. **Book vsaq11** (pendulum period same on all planets) vs. bank's Oscillations-unit row `ts_ipe_p1_osc_pendulum_clock_mountain` ("pendulum clock gain/lose time at a mountain top") — both hinge on T = 2π√(l/g) varying with g, but one is about altitude on one planet and the other about different planets; treated as distinct questions, NOT counted as an ELSEWHERE match.
