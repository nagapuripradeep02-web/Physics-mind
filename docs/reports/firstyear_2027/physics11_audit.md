# physics-11 examiner audit — wave B, 2026-09-03

12 newly authored cards audited. **23 findings: 5 HARMFUL, 8 WRONG, 7 WEAK, 3 REGISTER.**

Every automated gate was green first. Across all five chapters of wave B, **not one finding in 153
cards has been machine-checkable, and not one arithmetic answer has been wrong.**

## Four of the five harmful findings are the same pattern as physics-10's

A correction became a prohibition. Each of these told a student that a **correct** answer is an
error:

- **80 cal g⁻¹** for the latent heat of fusion of ice — correct, examiner-accepted, and the card's
  own printed lines never even offered it, so the student was told a right answer was wrong and given
  no way to write it.
- **C/100 = (F − 32)/180** — the identical relation before simplifying.
- **Converting both end temperatures to kelvin** before subtracting — 377 K − 297 K = 80 K gives the
  same answer.
- **dQ/dt ∝ (T − T₀)** where dQ/dt is defined as the rate of loss — a standard textbook form.

A fifth, close to the same shape: the card called listing the nature and area of the surface among
Newton's-law conditions an error. That is what the prescribed book itself lists, so an examiner
working from it will credit them. The physics distinction is right; calling it a student error is
exam-risky. The card now gives the genuine conditions and explains the other two as factors the rate
depends on, so the student covers the printed key without being taught a muddle.

**This is now the second chapter in a row where the wave's own corrections overshot.** The rule —
a correction says "write this", never "the other is wrong" unless it really is — belongs at the top
of the AUTHORING brief, not only the repair brief.

## The tip that modelled the error its own card warned against

The expansion-coefficient card warns against reversing the 5/9 fraction, and its memory tip then said
five ninths is "the fraction that turns a Celsius reading into Fahrenheit". It is the other way round.
A student reading the tip does exactly what the mistake line forbids.

## Physics corrected

- **A coefficient of expansion is a *fractional* change** per unit original length per degree, not
  simply "a change per degree".
- **Conduction and convection do not need a medium for the same reason.** Conduction passes heat from
  particle to particle without the material moving; convection needs a medium that can move and
  carries the heat with it. One card gave conduction's mechanism as the reason for both.
- **The fixed points need their pressure qualifier.** The scales card gave the boiling point with no
  mention of atmospheric pressure, while the pressure-cooker card in the same chapter teaches that
  the boiling point rises with pressure. Two cards in one chapter contradicting each other.
- **The minus sign in the cooling law.** Q is the body's heat content, so dQ/dt is negative while it
  cools and −dQ/dt is the positive rate of loss. One card justified the sign with a true statement
  that is not the reason, and the working form three steps later dropped the minus with no
  explanation of where it went.
- **The conductivity card asserted the formula the question told it to derive**, used K twice before
  defining it, spent a mark dividing the previous line by t, and hid its final arithmetic behind
  "multiplying and dividing through". All four are now fixed and the eight marks fall where the
  question asks.
- **Significant figures.** A card called rounding to 0.3 W a loss of precision the data supports, but
  the area is given to one significant figure.
