(printed_title "LIMITS") carries two `book_subsection` blocks: 36 "Limits" items (sv1–sv35, with sv27
split into two parts, sv27i/sv27ii, for the two one-sided limits) and 7 "Continuity" items (sa1–sa6,
plus a genuine duplicate-numbered second "3" recorded as sa3b — the book prints the number 3 twice in
the Continuity block, once on printed p.189/scan 55 and again on printed p.190/scan 56, so the block
holds 7 physical questions under labels 1–6). Our unit `mathematics_1b-8` ("Limits and Continuity")
already carries exactly these same 7 continuity cards under the same `sa*` refs, so this is not a case
of continuity being filed elsewhere or left uncovered — the book itself bundles continuity into the same
chapter, and our bank mirrors that 1:1. There are consequently zero EXTRA continuity cards.

**Every one of the 43 book-indexed questions has a bank card, matched ref-for-ref**, and in every case
but one the printed mathematics is character-for-character identical to our card's `text` (superscript
notation like `e⁷ˣ` vs. our `e^(7x)` is a rendering choice, not a math difference, and is not treated as
a mismatch). Both files were independently built against the same 43-item book index, which is why the
correspondence is total.

**sv20 — the one real discrepancy, and why it is still MATCHED, not MISSING.** The book prints
`lim(x → 2) (2x² − 7x − 4)/((2x − 1)(√x − 2))` on p.186 (scan p.52), and its own source-indexing note
already flags this as anomalous: at x = 2 the expression is *not* an indeterminate 0/0 form (direct
substitution already gives a finite value ≈ 5.69), which is completely out of pattern for the surrounding
0/0-technique exercise set, and the note explicitly says "the more usual textbook version of this
expression takes x → 4." Our card `ts_ipe_m1b_lim_2x2_minus_7x_minus_4_over_root_x_minus_2` cites this
exact book page and question number as its source and documents, in its own `verification.note`
("SOURCE STEM CORRECTED, NOT SILENTLY"), that it deliberately authors the limit at x → 4 — the point
where the numerator's `(x − 4)` factor and the denominator's `√x − 2` factor both vanish, producing the
genuine 0/0 form the factorise-and-rationalise technique (mark split: factorise+split, then cancel+
substitute) is built to resolve, with a numerically verified answer of 36/7. Two independent sources —
the book's own indexing note and our card's own verification note — agree the printed x → 2 is very
likely a misprint. Given the card explicitly targets this book row and documents the correction, this
is coverage of the intended question, not a gap: relisting it under MISSING would send the wrong signal
to whoever works the authoring queue (there is nothing left to author here). It is flagged with `*`
above and `needs_teacher_verification: true` is already set on the card so a Telangana IPE teacher can
confirm the corrected limit point against the physical book.

one-sided limit question 27 (right-hand and left-hand limits of `[x] + x` at x → 2) is split into
