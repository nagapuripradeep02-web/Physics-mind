## Notes

**Mechanical contract — all 25 pass.** `steps[].marks` sums to `marks_total` (1 + 1 = 2)
on every card; `mark_split` sums to 2 and its labels are identical, in order, to the step
labels; every step has `why`, `memory_tip`, `margin_note` and `common_mistakes`.
**No card in this unit carries an `insider_note`** — the deliberate policy for a chapter
with no exam history is intact across all 25.

**Mathematics re-derived, not trusted.** Independent checks made:
- AM/GM pairs: (20, 64) → 4, 16 and (68, 256) → 4, 64, both back-substituted into both means.
- `sum_1_2_2_3_3_4_n_terms`: n(n+1)(n+2)/3 tested at n = 1 (2) and n = 2 (8).
- `sum_0_7_0_77_0_777_n_terms`: (7/81)[9n − 1 + (1/10)ⁿ] tested at n = 1 → 0.7.
- `sum_8_88_888_n_terms`: (8/81)(10ⁿ⁺¹ − 9n − 10) tested at n = 1 → 8 and n = 2 → 96.
- `sum_2_3_5_6_8_9_2n_terms`: 3n² + 2n tested at n = 2 → 16 against 2 + 3 + 5 + 6.
- `infinite_sum_n_over_2_power`: S = 4 against the closed form 1/(1 − 1/2)² and the
  partial sum 3.5625 quoted in the card.
- `insert_five_am_between_8_and_26`: sequence listed — 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26, arithmetic
  with d = 3, confirmed 7 terms.
- `insert_three_gm_between_1_and_81`: both branches listed — 1, 3, 9, 27, 81 and
  1, −3, 9, −27, 81; both are genuine G.P.s ending at 81.
- `gp_which_term_is_320`: terms written out to t₇ = 320 with r = −2.
- Both componendo-and-dividendo proofs (`am_gm_ratio_m_n`, `sum_6_times_gm_ratio`) were
  re-run rule-by-rule, and `sum_6_times_gm_ratio` was verified numerically at
  a = 3 + 2√2, b = 3 − 2√2 (a + b = 6, √(ab) = 1).

**Infinite-GP condition.** All four infinite-sum cards
(`infinite_gp_one_third_ratio`, `infinite_gp_one_third_minus_two_ninths`,
`infinite_gp_sum_one_third_first_one_fourth`, `infinite_sum_n_over_2_power`) state
|r| < 1 explicitly before or beside the formula. This is the strongest part of the unit.

**One unstated hypothesis, not reported as a finding.** `ts_ipe_m1a_ss_am_gm_ratio_m_n`
takes √((m + n)/(m − n)) and the positive square root of
(√a + √b)²/(√a − √b)² without saying that m > n (which follows from A.M. ≥ G.M.) or that
a > b. Every printed textbook treatment glosses this the same way and no student is misled
by the omission, so it is recorded here rather than as a WEAK row. If the unit is ever
revisited, one clause in `s1_componendo`'s `why` ("A.M. ≥ G.M. guarantees m ≥ n, so the
root is real") would close it.

**Pattern.** The unit matches the group-level finding from A01/A02/A05: the mathematics is
right, the attached prose is where the defects sit. All 10 findings here are in
`common_mistakes`, `memory_tip` or `margin_note`; not one is in a printed working line.
Five of the ten are the same shape — a `common_mistakes` entry that condemns a
mathematically correct alternative route or form (a, ar, ar², ar³, ar⁴; b = 68 − a;
(y + z)(y² − yz + z²); a, ar, ar²; the decimal 1.5). Since these fields are read verbatim
to students by the chatbot, this unit tells a student five separate times that a correct
answer is a mistake.
