## Notes

- **The disclosed limit-point correction is right.** `ts_ipe_m1b_lim_2x2_minus_7x_minus_4_over_root_x_minus_2` moves the source's printed x → 2 to x → 4. Re-derived independently: at x = 2 the expression is (8 − 14 − 4)/[3(√2 − 2)] = −10/(−1.7574) ≈ 5.6904, a plain finite substitution with no indeterminate form, so no limit technique would be tested. At x = 4 the numerator (2x + 1)(x − 4) and the denominator factor √x − 2 both vanish, giving 0/0, and the cancellation yields (9)(4)/7 = 36/7 ≈ 5.14286. The correction is mathematically necessary and is disclosed in full in `verification.note`, in the stem and in the working. Not a defect.
- **Cross-card boilerplate: none material.** A mechanical sweep of every `memory_tip`, `margin_note`, `why`, `common_mistakes` line and `insider_note` across all 22 cards found exactly one string repeated between two different cards — "Dividing only the numerator by x and forgetting the denominator", shared by `lim_3x_minus_1_over_root_1_plus_x_minus_1` and `lim_ex_minus_1_over_root_1_plus_x_minus_1`. Those two questions are genuinely the same question with a different base; their steps are correctly differentiated (logₑ3 vs 1, 2logₑ3 vs 2) and their memory tips differ. Not a clone defect.
- **No prose field contradicts its own steps.** Every `insider_note` was diffed against its card's steps; all 22 agree, including the two "the answer is NO" cards (`con_sin_2x_over_x_at_zero`, `con_piecewise_at_2`) where the insider_note correctly leads with the negative verdict, and `con_k_squared_x_minus_k_find_k` where "x = 1 belongs to the FIRST piece" matches s2's "x = 1 satisfies x ≥ 1".
- **Line-follows-from-line check passed on all 22 cards**, including the two algebra-dense ones — `lim_cos_ax_minus_cos_bx_over_x2` s2 and `con_cos_ax_minus_cos_bx_at_zero` s3, where the multiply-and-divide line was expanded and confirmed to reproduce the original expression exactly (2·sinA·sinB/x²).
