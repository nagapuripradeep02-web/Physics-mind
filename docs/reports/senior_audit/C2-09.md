## HARMFUL — wrong chemistry a student would copy into an exam

_(none)_

Every printed line and every boxed answer in the group is chemically correct. The re-derivations
that matter: sodium stearate C₁₇H₃₅COONa is the C18 soap and the stearate ion carries head + tail;
`2AsH₃ → 2As + 3H₂` is balanced and arsenic is genuinely on the product side (and Mn²⁺ from
oxalic acid / acidified KMnO₄ is the second standard autocatalysis case); gold number = minimum mg
of protective colloid added to 10 mL of standard gold sol against 1 mL of 10% NaCl, with the red →
blue change as the sign of coagulation, and gelatin 0.005–0.01 vs starch ≈ 25 (NCERT table values,
inverse relation stated correctly); collodion = 4% cellulose nitrate in alcohol + ether; the five
coagulation methods (electrolyte, boiling, persistent dialysis, electrophoresis, mixing oppositely
charged sols) match NCERT exactly, as do the o/w agents (soaps, detergents, proteins, gums) and
w/o agents (heavy-metal salts of fatty acids, long-chain alcohols); milk = o/w, butter = w/o;
colloid range 1 nm–1000 nm = 10⁻⁹ m–10⁻⁶ m, passing filter paper but not a parchment/cellophane
membrane; amylase → maltose, pepsin → peptides, maltase → glucose; Mo promotes and CO poisons the
iron catalyst of the Haber process. All four `insider_note` claims that assert a chemical fact
(the stearate formula, the two gold-number volumes, the 1 nm–1000 nm range, Mo/CO) are true.

## Teacher-gate questions

1. **The 1 + 1 mark split on all 12 cards.** Every `verification_note` in this group states the split
   is the bank's own claim, not the board's, and `appearances` is empty on all 12 (no board
   back-test was possible — the TSBIE Basic Learning Material on hand is physics-only and
   first-year). A Telangana IPE teacher must confirm that Section-A 2-mark VSAQs in Chemistry-II
   are marked as definition (1) + example/reason (1) rather than as a single 2-mark block. This
   also decides whether the "half of this mark" wording in `ts_ipe_c2_sc_micelle_definition`
   s1_definition `margin_note` is meaningful — it is the only card in the group that refers to a
   half mark.
2. **Coagulation vs flocculation** (`ts_ipe_c2_sc_coagulation` s1_definition): the card states
   "It is also called flocculation or precipitation of the sol." NCERT uses "coagulation or
   precipitation"; some Indian texts reserve *flocculation* for the loose, reversible aggregation
   of a lyophilic sol. Confirm the board accepts them as synonyms in a 1-mark definition.
3. **The catalytic-poison example** (`ts_ipe_c2_sc_promoters_and_poisons` s2_poison): the card
   follows the source book with CO poisoning the iron catalyst in the Haber process (chemically
   sound and industrially standard). NCERT's own printed example is arsenic poisoning the platinum
   catalyst in the contact process. Confirm which the board expects, or that either is accepted.
4. **Kraft temperature** (`ts_ipe_c2_sc_micelle_definition` s1_definition): the card gives only the
   critical micelle concentration as the condition for micelle formation. NCERT states two
   conditions — above the Kraft temperature **and** above the CMC. Confirm whether a 1-mark
   definition is expected to name the temperature condition as well. Not filed as a finding: the
   card is not wrong, only shorter than NCERT, and the sibling card
   `ts_ipe_c2_sc_cmc_and_kraft_temperature` (group part 2/2) may already carry it.
5. **The absolute "no Tyndall effect in a true solution"** (`ts_ipe_c2_sc_tyndall_effect` s2
   `common_mistakes[0]`): the card deliberately teaches the absolute form and flags it as the exam
   answer ("In the exam answer a true solution shows no Tyndall effect"), which is honest but is a
   simplification — true solutions scatter very weakly. Confirm the board marks the absolute
   statement correct.
