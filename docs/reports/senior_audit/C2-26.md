## Teacher-gate questions

1. **Antiseptic vs disinfectant — substance or concentration?** NCERT says the same substance acts
   as either by varying the concentration. Two cards here (`dettol_constituents`,
   `tincture_of_iodine`) state a flat substance-level exclusion ("it is **not** a disinfectant").
   Does the Telangana board mark "Dettol is a disinfectant" wrong outright, or accept it with the
   concentration qualifier? The proposed rewording above assumes the latter.
2. **Is "table salt" alone acceptable** as a preservative example, or does the board want "sodium
   chloride (NaCl)" beside it? The source book prints "Table salt".
3. **Is the preservative/antioxidant boundary the board marks as hard as this unit teaches it?**
   Both cards assert "a preservative acts against micro-organisms; an antioxidant acts against
   oxygen", while the textbook's own antioxidant section describes antioxidants as helping in
   preservation. If the board treats antioxidants as a sub-class of preservatives, the two
   `common_mistakes` items that call BHA/BHT "not preservatives" would need softening.
4. **Codeine — natural alkaloid or morphine derivative?** `analgesics_classification` s3 `why`
   says heroin and codeine "are made from" morphine, while the card's own `verification` field
   says codeine is an alkaloid obtained from the opium poppy. Both are defensible (codeine occurs
   in opium and is also made industrially by methylating morphine), so this is not filed as a
   finding, but the card states it two ways and a teacher should pick one.
5. **"Synthetic detergent" = anionic only?** If the board's expected answer for this VSAQ is the
   anionic definition alone, the WEAK row above can be closed as convention rather than fixed.
6. **Spelling: bithionol vs bithional.** NCERT prints "bithional" alongside the name; this book and
   these cards use "bithionol". Recorded as a convention, not a finding.
