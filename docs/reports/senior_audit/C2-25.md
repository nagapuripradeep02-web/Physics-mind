## Teacher-gate questions

1. **Serotonin as a tranquiliser example.** The source book prints "Ex: Valium and serotonin" (p.105,
   VSAQ 3). The card refuses serotonin (correctly — it is a neurotransmitter, not a manufactured drug)
   and its `insider_note` states flatly that "writing it costs the example mark". If the board's key
   follows the book, serotonin may be accepted. A Telangana IPE chemistry teacher should rule on
   whether the insider note's prediction is safe, or whether it should be softened to "serotonin is not
   a drug; write valium instead, which is correct either way".
2. **Do cimetidine and ranitidine count as antacids on this board?** NCERT places them in the
   *Antacids* section; the bank puts them on both the antacid and the antihistamine card. If the board
   treats them as antihistamines only, the antacid card's last two lines should move.
3. **Which antibiotic definition does the key accept?** The book prints the older "produced by
   micro-organisms" wording; the card writes NCERT's modified "produced wholly or partly by chemical
   synthesis". A student following the card writes something the book-derived key may not contain
   verbatim. Confirm the board accepts the NCERT form (it should — it is the syllabus text).
4. **House spelling** for tranquiliser / tranquilliser / tranquilizer, and for the question stem
   "anti fertility drugs" (book) vs "antifertility drugs" (the card's own lines). Not a marks issue,
   but the bank should be internally consistent.

## Notes

- **Chemistry is in good shape.** Every factual claim in the group was checked against NCERT Class 12
  Ch. 16 and all but the one HARMFUL line is correct, including the ones the cards added over the
  source book: 0.2 % phenol antiseptic ↔ 1 % phenol disinfectant (NCERT verbatim), chlorine
  0.2–0.4 ppm and SO₂ in very low concentration as disinfectants, bithionol added to soaps,
  dettol = chloroxylenol + terpineol class, tincture of iodine and iodoform as antiseptics,
  formalin ≈ 40 % formaldehyde, saccharin ≈ 550× cane sugar and excreted unchanged, aspartame unstable
  at cooking temperature (cold food and drinks only), sucralose stable at cooking heat, alitame as the
  fourth sweetener, norethindrone = progesterone derivative, ethynylestradiol (novestrol) = oestrogen
  derivative and the pill a mixture of the two, brompheniramine (Dimetapp) and terfenadine (Seldane)
  as the allergy-receptor antihistamines, penicillin G narrow spectrum vs chloramphenicol broad
  spectrum, ampicillin and amoxycillin as broad-spectrum penicillins, veronal/amytal/nembutal/
  luminal/seconal as the barbiturates, and salvarsan and prontosil as antimicrobials.
- **The three source-book corrections the cards claim were each re-derived and are right**: the
  book's antihistamine definition covering only stomach receptors while its example list mixes both
  kinds; serotonin printed as a tranquiliser drug; and "artificial sweeteners have no calories", which
  is false of aspartame (a methyl ester of a dipeptide, digested like any peptide) — the card's
  "little or no food energy" is the correct wording, and its `insider_note` on that point is sound.
  The book's undifferentiated antiseptic/disinfectant definitions are also genuinely undifferentiated,
  and the "applied on living tissue" ↔ "applied on a non-living surface" split the cards add is NCERT's.
- **Structure: 10/10 clean.** Every card is 1 + 1 = 2 = `marks_total`; every `mark_split` label is
  character-identical to its step's label; every `mark_note` ("Definition"/"Examples"/"Example")
  describes work the step does; `expected_time_min` = 4 on all ten, consistent across the group.
  No 0-mark steps, no figures, no `recall` block anywhere in the group.
- **The antiseptic ↔ disinfectant mirror prose was checked and is NOT filed.** Five strings are shared
  or near-identical across the pair (`common_mistakes[0]` on both s1 steps, the boxed
  "Phenol: 0.2% antiseptic, 1% disinfectant" line, "The concentration is what makes the statement
  true", the s2 label). Each is accurate for the card it sits on and the pair is a deliberate contrast
  pair — the duplication is the point, not a defect.
- **The `insider_note` register across the group** leans on unsourced claims about student and
  examiner behaviour ("The definition mark is lost more often than the example mark",
  "Most students write only two drug names here", "the point the examiner looks for"). Only the
  barbiturates one is filed, because it invents a 4-mark form of a question sourced as a 2-mark VSAQ;
  the rest are the bank's established house style and are harmless, but they are claims no one has
  verified and a teacher gate would be the place to settle whether they should stay.
- **The one equation in the group is balanced** and its products are right (NaHCO₃ is the salt of a
  weak acid, HCl the stomach acid; NaCl + H₂O + CO₂ are the correct products, and the CO₂ is why
  excess hydrogencarbonate is uncomfortable as well as alkalising).
