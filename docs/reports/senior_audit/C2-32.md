## Teacher-gate questions

1. **`benzenesulphonamide` vs `N-phenylbenzenesulphonamide`** (`ts_ipe_c2_ocn_conversions_ethylation_and_sulphonamide`). The card is chemically right — aniline gives C₆H₅SO₂NHC₆H₅ — but the sourced question asks for "benzenesulphonamide", and the card's mistakes line then calls that name an error. If the Telangana board itself sets the question in those words, the mistakes line needs softening to "the board's wording is loose; write N-phenylbenzenesulphonamide and you cannot be marked down", rather than flat condemnation.
2. **Hinsberg test with a 3° amine** (`ts_ipe_c2_ocn_hinsberg_test_distinguishing_amines`, s4). The card follows NCERT/board: "no reaction". Modern texts describe an unstable quaternary ammonium salt that hydrolyses back, i.e. no isolable product rather than no reaction. Confirm the board wants the flat "does not react".
3. **HVZ carried to di- and trichloroacetic acid** (`ts_ipe_c2_ocn_fourteen_named_reactions`, s3, part v). NCERT shows only α-mono-halogenation under Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky conditions; the source book prints the full three-step chain to Cl₃C–COOH, which the card reproduces (correctly balanced, and with the book's `CH₂Cl₂–COOH` typo fixed to CHCl₂–COOH). Confirm the board accepts the three-step chain as HVZ.
4. **Cu₂Cl₂ vs CuCl** (`ts_ipe_c2_ocn_sandmeyer_and_gattermann_reactions`). The card writes cuprous chloride as Cu₂Cl₂ (the Indian-text dimer convention, and NCERT's) but writes CuCl as the by-product of the CuCN equation in the same step. Both forms are standard; confirm which the board expects, and whether the mixed usage inside one step is acceptable.
5. **The reason p-toluenesulphonyl chloride replaces the benzene reagent** (`ts_ipe_c2_ocn_hinsberg_test_distinguishing_amines`, s4): the card says "because it is easier to purify"; the usual textbook reason is "because it is a solid and is easier to handle". Both are defensible for a crystalline solid; confirm the wording the board's scheme rewards.
6. **Every mark split in this group is the bank's claim, not the board's.** Each `verification.note` states that no second-year chemistry paper is in the corpus and that no two-book union check was possible (the TSBIE Basic Learning Material on hand is physics-only and first-year). The 2+2, 1+1+1+1 and 2+2+2+2 splits — and especially the authored four-block split of the fourteen-reaction LAQ — need a Telangana IPE chemistry teacher's confirmation. Nothing in this audit can substitute for that.

## Notes

- **Chemically this group is in very good shape.** 44/44 equations balance; every reagent, condition,
  temperature, product name and trend order checks out against NCERT Class 12. Zero HARMFUL findings,
  and the two WRONGs are both in explanatory prose, not in a line copied onto the answer sheet.
- **The source-book corrections are themselves correct.** Each was re-derived independently:
  (a) `C₃H₇Cl + 2NH₃ → C₃H₇NH₂ + NH₄Cl` and `C₂H₅Cl + NaCN → C₂H₅CN + NaCl` (book left Cl and Na out)
  — both now balance; (b) the Hinsberg 1° scheme rewritten with one R and one H on nitrogen (the book
  printed the 2° drawing twice) — and the surviving N–H is indeed what makes the product dissolve in
  alkali; (c) `C₂H₅Cl + KCN → C₂H₅CN + KCl` / `C₂H₅Cl + AgCN → C₂H₅NC + AgCl` (book omitted both metal
  halides); (d) Pd/BaSO₄ correctly separated from Lindlar's catalyst (Pd/CaCO₃ + quinoline + lead
  acetate, for alkynes) in the Rosenmund entry; (e) the second HVZ product corrected from the book's
  `CH₂Cl₂–COOH` to CHCl₂–COOH, which the third step then requires; (f) "alkanediazonium" for the book's
  "di-azonium". All six corrections stand.
- **The added numbers are right.** pKb 9.38 / 3.38, electronegativity O ≈ 3.5 vs N ≈ 3.0, ethanol pKa
  ≈ 16, 273–283 K for diazotisation, 453–473 K for the Wolff-Kishner stage — all standard NCERT
  Class 12 values. The ethylamine order added to the basicity card, (C₂H₅)₂NH > (C₂H₅)₃N > C₂H₅NH₂ >
  NH₃, reproduces NCERT's own pKb table (3.00, 3.25, 3.29, 4.75) exactly and is a genuine improvement
  on the book, which states the methyl order as though it were general.
- **Rule 41: clean.** A scripted idiom/personification sweep over every reader-facing string (lines,
  `why`, `memory_tip`, `margin_note`, `common_mistakes`, `insider_note`, `question_text`) returned
  zero hits. The chemistry register ("the lone pair is less available to a proton", "the carbonyl
  carbon leaves as carbonate", "sodium joins two carbon skeletons") is textbook vocabulary, not
  metaphor.
- **`mark_split` labels are longer than step labels throughout the unit** (e.g. "Cyanide and
  isocyanide on reduction" ↔ step label "Cyanide and isocyanide"). Every pair maps to the right step
  in order and every `marks` value matches. This is a unit convention, not a defect — not filed.
- **Pattern worth a decision, not filed as six rows:** seven reader-facing strings across six cards
  refer to the source book ("the book prints", "both printed equations in the book are unbalanced",
  "the book's own answer"). Two of them are load-bearing enough to file (above). The rest —
  `prepare_dimethylpropanamine…` insider_note, `conversions_ethylation…` insider_note,
  `hinsberg_test…` insider_note, `ethyl_cyanide…` insider_note — are arguably a feature, since the
  FAST TRACK guide is the book most of these students own and the warnings are accurate. A single
  house rule is needed: either book-provenance belongs only in `verification.note`, or an
  `insider_note` may name the guide. Today it is done both ways within one unit.
- Minor, not filed as findings: (a) the Sandmeyer nitrile equation carries CuCN as a reactant **and**
  `[CuCN / KCN]` as its condition bracket, so a student copying both writes CuCN twice; (b) the two
  memory tips "K for potassium, C for carbon" and "Ag for silver, N for nitrogen"
  (`ethyl_cyanide…` s2/s3) look like letter mnemonics but the letters carry no link — each is
  rescued by the real reason stated in the same sentence; (c) `amines_less_acidic_than_alcohols`
  uses "amide ion" for R–NH⁻ in s2 and "amide" for R–CONH₂ in s4 — both standard, but the collision
  sits inside one card; (d) `expected_time_min` is 8 for every 4-mark SAQ and 15 for the 8-mark LAQ,
  consistent at ~2 min/mark across the group.
