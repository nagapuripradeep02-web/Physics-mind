# C2-18 — Chemistry-II, Unit 11 d and f Block Elements (part 1/2)

Cards audited: 10 / 10   Findings: HARMFUL 0 · WRONG 6 · WEAK 5

Every card was solved from `QUESTION:` alone before its steps were read. A python script
(`scratchpad/C2-18/check.py`) rebuilt the ground-state configurations of Sc–Zn from Z (18 + 3d + 4s
== Z for all ten), removed 4s before 3d to form each ion, counted unpaired electrons under Hund's
rule and computed μ = √(n(n+2)) — every configuration, every unpaired count and all three quoted
moments (√35 = 5.9161 → 5.92; √24 = 4.8990 → 4.90; √3 = 1.7321 → 1.73) are correct. The same script
ran the transition-element test over Sc–Zn (Sc–Cu all pass, Zn alone fails — as the cards claim),
charge-balanced the two linkage isomers ([Co(NO₂)(NH₃)₅]²⁺ and [Co(ONO)(NH₃)₅]²⁺ both come out 2+
with Co(III)), sized the en chelate ring (M–N–C–C–N = 5), and summed both alloy compositions
(60+25+15 = 100; 95+5 = 100). All ten cards are 1 + 1 = 2 = `marks_total`, `expected_time_min` = 4
throughout, and the raw JSON was checked for hidden fields — these cards carry no `recall` rubric
and no figures, so checks 5 (figures) and the `recall` half of check 3 are N/A for this group.

`..._chelate_ligand` (the book's printed definition really does define a polydentate ligand).

## Teacher-gate questions

1. **Ambidentate ligand names.** The card writes SCN⁻ as "thiocyanato-S / thiocyanato-N"; current
   NCERT Class 12 (ch. 9) prints "thiocyanato" (S-bonded) and "**iso**thiocyanato" (N-bonded). Both
   are in circulation in Indian texts. Which does a Telangana IPE examiner expect? Same question for
   NO₂⁻: the card uses the current NCERT "nitrito-N / nitrito-O", while many Telangana guides still
   print the older "nitro / nitrito". Recorded as convention, not filed as an error.
2. **Anhydrous CuSO₄.** The source book (and the usual board key) says "in the absence of water,
   crystal field splitting is not possible". The card deliberately corrects this to the accurate
   version — sulphate oxygens still split the d set, but so weakly that Δo falls below the visible
   range. The correction is right, but a key-matching examiner may be looking for the book's
   sentence. Should the card add the book's phrasing as an accepted alternative?
3. **Aqua-copper(II) formula.** The card says the gap is "produced by the **six** water ligands"
   (octahedral t2g/eg). NCERT's colour table writes the species as [Cu(H₂O)₄]²⁺. Which formula
   should the card use when it names a coordination number?
4. **Nichrome composition.** The card keeps the book's 60 % Ni / 25 % Fe / 15 % Cr (sums to 100) and
   hedges it as "a composition, not the composition", naming the 80 Ni / 20 Cr grade. Does the board
   key demand the 60/25/15 figures specifically?
5. **"First transition series" including Zn.** `..._zn2_diamagnetic_mn2_paramagnetic` calls Zn²⁺ an
   ion at one end of "the first transition series", while `..._scandium_transition_zinc_not` and
   `..._why_called_transition_elements` state that zinc is **not** a transition element. Both usages
   are standard (Sc–Zn as a series by position; Sc–Cu by the strict definition), but the two cards
   sit in one unit and a student may read them as contradicting each other. Is the loose usage
   acceptable in an answer, or should the cards say "the first d-block series" in the colour/magnetism
   cards?
6. Every card's `verification_note` records that the 1 + 1 split is the bank's **claim**, not the
   board's, and that no second-year chemistry paper and no second book were available for a
   cross-check. That gap stands for all ten cards.

## Notes

- **The chemistry of this group is sound.** Zero wrong configurations, zero wrong unpaired counts,
  zero wrong moments, zero wrong verdicts, zero wrong compositions, zero charge-imbalance. Nothing
  in the group is a chemical equation, so the atom/charge-balance sweep reduced to the two complex
  ions, which balance.
- **The one repeated chemical defect is the d⁹ promotion**, and it appears on both copper cards
  (`..._hydrated_copper_sulphate_colour` s1 and `..._aqueous_cu2_blue_zn2_colourless` s1). It is a
  common textbook shorthand rather than an invention of this bank, and no IPE examiner would deduct
  for it, which is why it is filed WRONG and not HARMFUL — but it is the one place the group teaches
  a mechanism that is not what happens.
- **The second repeated defect is a margin note naming a term the step never writes** (`d-d
  transition`, on the same two cards) — a `margin_note` describing work the printed answer does not
  do. Worth a single sweep over the unit rather than two separate edits.
- **`insider_note`: 10 / 10 present**, one per card, and nine of them are true for their card and
  consistent with the mark split. The one exception (`..._hydrated_copper_sulphate_colour`, "half a
  mark") is filed WEAK. No insider note is chemically false.
- **Marks:** every card sums 1 + 1 = 2 = `marks_total`; every `mark_split` entry pairs with its step
  in order; every `mark_note` describes work the step does. `mark_split` labels are paraphrases of
  the step labels rather than copies — that is the convention across all ten cards, so it is not
  filed. `expected_time_min` = 4 on all ten, consistent for a 2-mark VSAQ.
- **Source-book corrections re-derived and confirmed right:** (a) the copper case defeats the book's
  "unpaired d electron in the penultimate shell" criterion, as the card says — Cu is 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ with no
  unpaired d electron, yet Cu²⁺ is 3d⁹; (b) the book's chelate definition really does define a
  polydentate ligand, and the card's added "same metal ion / closes a ring" is the missing condition;
  (c) the book's "no crystal field splitting in anhydrous CuSO₄" really is too strong.
- **Not filed, but noted for a later pass:** (i) `..._mischmetal` s2 lists three "uses" of which the
  first (added to magnesium-based alloys) is the vehicle for the other two (bullets/shells, lighter
  flint) rather than a parallel use — the source prints them as one phrase, and the split is
  defensible, but the boxed claim "the property all three uses depend on" sits oddly beside the
  `why`'s "which is a separate benefit"; (ii) `..._alloy_definition` s1 `why` says an alloy has "no
  sharp melting point", which is the standard textbook line but not true of eutectic alloys;
  (iii) `..._hydrated_copper_sulphate_colour` s1 says "the water molecules act as ligands around
  Cu²⁺" — in the pentahydrate four of the five waters are coordinated and the fifth is lattice water,
  which is beyond board scope; (iv) `..._spin_only_magnetic_moment_fe2` s2 says the measured moment
  is "a little above 4.90 BM" — the observed range for high-spin Fe²⁺(aq) is about 5.1–5.5 BM, so
  "a little" is generous but not wrong.
- **Rule 41:** no idiom, no metaphor and no personification found in any reader-facing string. The
  nearest approaches are `..._spin_only_magnetic_moment_fe2` s1 `memory_tip` ("six electrons in five
  boxes leaves four alone" — orbital boxes are standard notation, "alone" reads as "unpaired") and
  `..._zn2_diamagnetic_mn2_paramagnetic` s2 `margin_note` ("extra credit"); neither is filed.
- These cards carry **no `recall` rubric and no figures** (confirmed against the raw JSON — the step
  keys are id/kind/label/marks/lines/why/common_mistakes/memory_tip/margin_note only), so there is no
  grader-side rubric in this group that could reject a correct student answer.
