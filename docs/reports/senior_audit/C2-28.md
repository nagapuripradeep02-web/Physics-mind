Cards audited: 9 / 9   Findings: HARMFUL 0 · WRONG 2 · WEAK 5

Every card was solved from its `QUESTION:` line before the steps were read. All 17 printed chemical
equations were atom- and charge-balanced by a python script (`balance.py`: element Counters per side,
including the `[O]` nascent-oxygen forms, the two Grignard stages, the Dow two-NaOH form and all four
ether cleavages) — **17/17 PASS**, and the book's unbalanced 1-Br₂ tribromination was run as a negative

quoted temperature/boiling point/melting point were recomputed or checked against standard values. A
second script (`structure.py`) checked mark sums, `mark_split` ↔ step label/marks pairing, `boxed_final`
placement and cross-card prose duplication for all 9 cards — all clean, zero duplicated prose. The one
value that could not be settled from the card alone, the Dow-process pressure, was checked against
NCERT directly; it is the group's single substantive finding.

## Teacher-gate questions

1. **300 atm or 320 atm for the chlorobenzene fusion?** NCERT Class 12 prints 320 atm in §11.4.2
   (Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers — the chapter this question sits in) and 300 atm in the Haloalkanes and
   Haloarenes chapter; the source book itself prints 300 atm here and 320 atm at its own p.111. A
   Telangana IPE teacher must rule on which the board's key expects and whether the other is accepted.
   The ruling has a second consequence outside this group: `ts_ipe_c2_ape_two_preparations_of_phenol`
   files "Writing 320 atm, which some books print for this question. The standard value is 300 atm" as a
   student **mistake** — if the board follows §11.4.2 that entry condemns a correct answer.
2. **Tertiary alcohol under strong oxidation.** NCERT's sentence is "cleavage of various C–C bonds takes
   place and a mixture of **carboxylic acids** containing lesser number of carbon atoms is formed"; this
   card (`..._reagents_for_oxidation_of_primary_alcohol`, s2 `why`) says "a mixture of smaller **ketones
   and acids**". The card is chemically right for a tertiary alcohol whose alkene cleaves to a ketone
   (2-methylbutan-2-ol → acetone + acetic acid), but it is not NCERT's wording. Is "ketones" accepted?
   Related: the same step files "Saying a tertiary alcohol can never be oxidised" as a mistake, which is
   NCERT's own unqualified first sentence before its strong-conditions caveat.
3. **Cumene air-oxidation temperature.** The card writes "about 373 K"; NCERT gives no temperature for
   this step. Is a temperature expected, and is 373 K the value the board's key carries?
4. **"Chelation"** is offered as a synonym for intramolecular hydrogen bonding (`..._steam_distillation`,
   s2). Standard in Indian texts; confirm the board's key accepts it as the named reason.
5. **Every mark split in this group is the bank's claim, not the board's** — each card's `verification`
   says so, and no second-year chemistry board paper is in the corpus for a back-test. The 1+1 (VSAQ)
   and 1+1+1+1 (SAQ) shapes still need a Telangana teacher's confirmation.
