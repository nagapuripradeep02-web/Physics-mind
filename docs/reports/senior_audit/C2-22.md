## WRONG — incorrect but not exam-costing

| question_id | step_id | what is wrong | what it should say |
|---|---|---|---|
| ts_ipe_c2_bio_glycosidic_linkage | s1_definition | `why`: "Naming it an ether linkage tells a student what kind of bond it is and **why dilute acid hydrolyses it back to the two sugars**." The stated cause is false — ethers are notably *resistant* to hydrolysis by dilute acid, which is why they are used as inert solvents. The glycosidic bond hydrolyses easily because C-1 is an **acetal** carbon: it carries the ring oxygen *and* the bridging oxygen, and acetals are hydrolysed by dilute acid. As written the sentence teaches the opposite of the real reason. | "…the bridge that is left is an oxygen atom joining two carbon atoms. The bridging carbon, C-1, already carries the ring oxygen, so it is an acetal carbon — and that is why dilute acid hydrolyses the linkage back to the two sugars." |
| ts_ipe_c2_bio_glycosidic_linkage | s2_where | `why`: "…the α or β label … is **the only structural difference between starch and cellulose**." False, and contradicted by this group's own `ts_ipe_c2_bio_glycogen_and_starch`, which states that starch is a *mixture of two polymers*, amylose (linear) and amylopectin (branched, with C₁–C₆ branch points), while cellulose is a single unbranched polymer. Branching is a second, larger structural difference. | "The α or β label is fixed by the configuration at the anomeric carbon of the first sugar, and it is the difference in the linkage that makes cellulose's chains straight and starch's coiled." (Drop "the only".) |
| ts_ipe_c2_bio_glycogen_and_starch | s1_glycogen | `why` (two defects in one sentence): "it holds many glucose units in **one insoluble molecule** and **release** them on demand". (i) Glycogen is water-**soluble** — it disperses to an opalescent colloidal solution, unlike starch granules; the reason a store is polymeric is that a polymer is osmotically almost inert, not that it is insoluble. (ii) Subject–verb agreement: "release" for "releases". | "Glycogen does in an animal what starch does in a plant: it locks many glucose units into one large molecule, which raises the osmotic pressure of the cell far less than the same glucose free in solution would, and releases them on demand." |
| ts_ipe_c2_bio_hydrolysis_of_sucrose_and_lactose | s1_sucrose | `common_mistakes[1]` (and the matching `margin_note`): "Leaving out the α and β labels. The printed answer key gives α-D-glucose and β-D-fructose." This condemns correct chemistry. NCERT's own sentence for this reaction is "on hydrolysis gives equimolar mixture of D-(+)-glucose and D-(−)-fructose" — no anomeric labels — and it is the more accurate statement, because the moment the two sugars are free they mutarotate to equilibrium anomeric mixtures; α and β describe the units *inside* sucrose, which the card's own `why` already says. A student who writes "glucose and fructose" has not made a mistake. | "Writing fructose as an aldose or glucose as the ketose. In sucrose the glucose unit is α and the fructose unit is β, and that is how the printed key labels them." — and soften the margin note to "Both products earn this mark; adding the α and β labels matches the printed key." |
| ts_ipe_c2_bio_effect_of_denaturation | s1_what_changes | The printed line "**Only** the hydrogen bonds are broken; the peptide bonds stay intact" contradicts the same step's `why`, which correctly says "the forces that hold the folded shape are **hydrogen bonds and other weak forces**". Denaturation also breaks hydrophobic interactions, salt bridges and (in many proteins) disulphide bridges. The word "Only" is what makes the line false. | "The weak forces that hold the folded shape — hydrogen bonds and the like — are broken; the peptide bonds stay intact." |

## Teacher-gate questions

1. **"Oxide linkage" vs "ether linkage" (`ts_ipe_c2_bio_glycosidic_linkage`).** NCERT Class 12 and
   the source book both write *oxide linkage*; this card deliberately replaces it with *ether
   linkage* (see its `VERIFICATION_NOTE`). Strictly neither is exact — the bond is an acetal — but a
   Telangana examiner may be reading for the printed words "oxide linkage". Does the board key accept
   "ether linkage", and should the card print both?
2. **α/β labels on sucrose hydrolysis products.** Does the TS IPE key award full marks for
   "glucose and fructose" (NCERT's wording), or does it require "α-D-glucose and β-D-fructose" as the
   source book prints? The WRONG row above assumes the former is acceptable; if the board genuinely
   penalises the unlabelled answer, the `common_mistakes` entry stands as written and only its
   justification needs rewording.
3. **Is item (iv) a "reaction"?** The question asks for *reactions* D-glucose's open chain cannot
   explain, and the second mark is spent on the existence of two crystalline forms — a property of
   the solid, not a reaction. The card's `VERIFICATION_NOTE` already flags this as the book's shape.
   Confirm the board accepts it inside a "reactions" answer (NCERT lists it under the same heading).
4. **Fibrous proteins: hydrogen bonds only?** `ts_ipe_c2_bio_globular_vs_fibrous_proteins` s2 says
   the parallel chains are "held together by hydrogen bonds"; NCERT says "held together by hydrogen
   and **disulphide** bonds". Not filed as a finding (an omission, not an error) — but if the board
   key prints "disulphide", the card is one word short of the mark.
