# C2-16 — Chemistry-II, Unit 9 VIIA Group Elements (part 1/1)

Cards audited: 13 / 13   Findings: HARMFUL 0 · WRONG 2 · WEAK 9

Every one of the 28 printed equations was parsed by a python script (`balance.py`) that expands
parentheses and sums atoms **and** charge on each side — 28/28 balance, including both aqua-regia
ionic equations (−1 and −6 on each side) and both electrode half-reactions (−2 each side). The
checker was proved to fire in both directions by a negative control (`checks2.py`) on three
deliberately broken forms, one of them the source book's own `4HCl + O₂ → 2Cl₂ + 3H₂O`, which it
flagged. Redox electron balance was recomputed for all 15 redox equations (e⁻ lost = e⁻ gained in
every one, including the two disproportionations and the thiosulphate +6/−2 split); all four
oxidation states of card 9 were re-solved from the charge equation (+1, +3, +5, +7); the oxoacid
lone-pair count (3, 2, 1, 0) and every VSEPR shape were re-derived from scratch; and every NCERT
Class-12 group-17 number quoted (electron gain enthalpies, bond dissociation enthalpies, X–X bond
lengths, hydrogen-halide boiling points) was checked against the NCERT table and re-sorted to
confirm the three boxed orders. A structural script (`struct.py`) confirmed all 13 mark sums,
all 13 `mark_split` ↔ step-label pairings, zero `recall` rubrics, and zero exact duplicate prose
within or across cards.

## Teacher-gate questions

1. **Bleaching powder formula (card `cl2_slaked_lime_nai`).** The card follows the source book:
   `Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂ → CaOCl₂ + H₂O`, "calcium oxychloride". NCERT Class 12 writes
   `2Ca(OH)₂ + 2Cl₂ → Ca(OCl)₂ + CaCl₂ + 2H₂O` and gives the composition
   Ca(OCl)₂·CaCl₂·Ca(OH)₂·2H₂O. Both are printed in Indian texts. Which does a Telangana IPE
   examiner mark, and is the other accepted?
2. **Single arrow for chlorine in water (card `f2_cl2_with_water`, s2 `common_mistakes[0]`).** The
   card calls `Cl₂ + H₂O → HCl + HOCl` with a single arrow a *mistake* and requires ⇌. The source
   book — and many Indian texts — print the single arrow. Is a single arrow actually penalised? If
   not, this item is condemning working the board accepts.
3. **Charge notation (card `aqua_regia`, s2 `common_mistakes[0]`).** The card calls `PtCl₆⁻²` a
   mistake and requires `PtCl₆²⁻`. IUPAC agrees with the card, but the book prints the other order
   and it is widely written that way in Telangana answer scripts. Is it penalised in practice?
4. **"HF is a liquid at room temperature" (card `hf_liquid_hcl_gas`).** The card's own number,
   293 K, is 19.9 °C — below the 298 K usually called room temperature, at which HF is (just) a
   gas. The board question itself asserts HF is a liquid, so the card is faithful to the paper;
   confirm the wording an examiner expects, since a sharp student can see the tension.
5. **Deacon's process inside the laboratory-preparation answer (card
   `chlorine_lab_preparation_reactions`).** The book lists Deacon's process as a third method under
   a question that asks for the *laboratory* preparation; the card deliberately drops it as
   industrial and says so in a margin note. Confirm an examiner does not expect it there.
6. **Nelson cell vs Castner–Kellner cell (card `chlorine_electrolytic_naoh_nh3`).** The card
   answers "electrolytic method" with the Nelson cell, following the book. Confirm the board does
   not expect the Castner–Kellner cell (or a labelled diagram — the card authors no figure).
7. **Every mark split in this unit is the bank's own claim.** All 13 verification notes say so, and
   no second-year chemistry board paper exists in the corpus to back-test against. The 1+1 VSAQ,
   1+1+1+1 / 1+1+2 SAQ and 2+1+1+1+1+1+1 / 2+2+1+1+1+1 LAQ shapes all need a teacher's sign-off.
8. **Interhalogen preparation temperatures (card `interhalogen_compounds`, s2).** The card prints
   `Cl₂ + F₂ → 2ClF (equal volumes)` and `Cl₂ + 3F₂ → 2ClF₃ (excess F₂)` without the NCERT
   temperatures 437 K and 573 K. Is a temperature expected on the equation?
