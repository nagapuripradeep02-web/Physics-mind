## Notes

- **The ionosphere correction is confirmed RIGHT (the task's specific check).** The source book
  places D and E in the "stratosphere"; `ts_ipe_p2_com_ionosphere_layers` corrects this to: D ≈
  60–90 km (upper mesosphere), E ≈ 90–120 km (lower thermosphere), F₁ ≈ 150–200 km and F₂ > 200 km
  (both thermosphere), and states no ionosphere layer is in the stratosphere. Verified independently:
  the stratosphere runs to ≈ 50 km, and even the lowest ionosphere layer (D, bottom 60 km) starts
  10 km above that — so the book's original placement is physically impossible, and the card's
  correction direction is right. Layer-height ordering is monotonic and non-overlapping (D-top 90 =
  E-bottom 90; E-top 120 ≤ F1-bottom 150; F1-top 200 = F2-start 200) — internally consistent.
- **E₀/B₀ = c** (`ts_ipe_p2_emw_e0_b0_relation`) is the correct free-space EM-wave amplitude relation
  and the correct orientation (E on top); the card's rejection of the inverted ratio (which would be
  1/c ≈ 3.3×10⁻⁹, "a very small number") is right.
- **Displacement current = conduction current = 0.6 A** (`ts_ipe_p2_emw_displacement_current_capacitor`)
  is the standard Ampère–Maxwell-consistency result; no computation needed beyond the equality, which
  is correctly stated and correctly not miscalculated (no halving/doubling).
- **E = hc/λ doubling → energy halves**, **speech bandwidth 3100 − 300 = 2800 Hz**, and **IR:UV vacuum
  speed ratio = 1:1** were all confirmed by direct substitution/subtraction in the verify.py script.
- `ts_ipe_p2_emw_infrared_rays_use` is the only card in either group with no `[boxed]` final-answer
  line (both its steps are `kind=text`). This looks like a deliberate design choice rather than a
  defect: the question asks for "any one" of four valid book uses, so there is no single canonical
  value to box the way `ts_ipe_p2_emw_e0_b0_relation`'s `E₀/B₀ = c` or `ts_ipe_p2_com_speech_frequency_range`'s
  `2800 Hz` can be. Not filed as a finding, but flagged here in case the house convention intends
  every card to carry a boxed line regardless.
- Every `MISTAKE` entry in both groups was checked against the possibility of condemning correct
  physics (Method §3(a) of the brief) — none does. Several `MISTAKE` entries correctly distinguish a
  nearby-but-different-and-real value from the asked one without calling the alternative wrong in
  itself (e.g. `ts_ipe_p2_emw_xray_average_wavelength` s2's geometric-mean mistake is described as "a
  real mid-point of the range, but not the value asked for" — accurate, not a false condemnation).
