# C2-15 — Chemistry-II, Unit 8 VIA Group Elements (part 1/1)

Cards audited: 10 / 10   Findings: HARMFUL 0 · WRONG 3 · WEAK 8

Every card was solved from its `QUESTION:` line before its steps were read. A python parser
(`balance.py`, nested-parenthesis + hydrate-dot aware) atom-balanced all **18 printed equations**
— all 18 balance — and separately checked the **8 equations the cards quote as student errors**,
confirming each really is unbalanced or the wrong species, so no mistakes line condemns a valid
equation. Redox electron counts were balanced by hand for all six ozone/antichlor/contact
oxidations (S −2→+6 = 8 e⁻ ⇒ 4 O₃ for PbS; S +4→+6 = 2 e⁻ ⇒ 1 Cl₂; 2 Ag or 2 Hg 0→+1 = 2 e⁻ ⇒ 1
[O]). A second script (`structure.py`) checked every `mark_split` sum, label↔step pairing and step-
marks pairing (all 10 cards clean), and a third (`prose.py`) swept every reader-facing string for
duplicated sentences and Rule-41 register. Contact-process conditions (V₂O₅, 720 K, 2 bar,
ΔH = −196.6 kJ mol⁻¹, 96–98% pure), the group-16 hydride boiling points, the O₂ MO configuration
(16 e⁻, two π* singly occupied) and the O₃ bond angle 117° were all checked against NCERT Class 12
p-block values. Both figures were reconstructed from their stroke coordinates and are correct.

## Teacher-gate questions

1. **ΔH of ozone formation — the number a Telangana examiner expects.** This card prints
   `3O₂ ⇌ 2O₃, ΔH = +284 kJ` (and its `why` explains it as +142 kJ per mole of ozone, which is
   thermodynamically right: ΔH_f(O₃) ≈ +142.7 kJ mol⁻¹). NCERT Class 12 p-block prints
   **+142 kJ mol⁻¹ against that same equation**, and the source book printed "284.5 kJ/mole". Both
   readings are in circulation. A teacher must rule which form the board's key accepts, because a
   student writing +284 against 3O₂ → 2O₃ is arguing with the printed NCERT line.
2. **Cross-bank conflict the card itself flags.** This card uses NCERT contact-process figures
   (ΔH = −196.6 kJ mol⁻¹, 720 K, 2 bar); the Chemistry-I card
   `ts_ipe_c1_ce_lechatelier_industrial` gives −189 kJ at ~673 K and 1.5–1.7 atm for the same
   reaction. Not edited from this desk. The two must be reconciled together.
3. **Hg₂O.** Indian state texts write the tailing reaction as 2Hg + O₃ → Hg₂O + O₂ and the card
   follows them (and its mistakes line rejects HgO on balance grounds, correctly). Mercury(I)
   oxide is not a well-characterised compound in modern inorganic chemistry. Confirm Hg₂O is what
   the board's key wants before anyone "corrects" it to HgO.
4. **Group 16 membership.** The card fixes the group as O, S, Se, Te, Po and files omitting
   polonium as a mistake. Recent NCERT printings also name livermorium (Lv). Should Lv be
   accepted / expected?
5. **Colour of Ag₂O.** The card writes "black silver oxide" for the ozone-tarnishing of silver.
   Ag₂O is usually described as brown-black. Confirm the wording the board accepts.
6. **Half marks.** The `drying_ammonia` finding above assumes IPE does not split a 1-mark VSAQ
   item. Confirm.

- **Both figures verified from their stroke coordinates.** `sf4_see_saw`: two axial bonds up and
  down (both leaning right, away from the lone pair, as its `why` claims), two equatorial bonds

  `sf6_octahedron`: four bonds in a plus, one solid oblique to the front-lower-left and one dashed

  `verification` note says are wrong (the book draws SF₆ as a flat hexagon), and the card's
  correction is itself right.
