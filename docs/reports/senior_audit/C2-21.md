## HARMFUL — wrong chemistry a student would copy into an exam

_(none)_

Every boxed answer, monomer, repeat unit and reagent in this group is chemically correct. The
re-derivations that matter, all machine-confirmed: (C₂H₅)₃Al = C₆H₁₅Al with **one** Al;
glycine + 6-aminohexanoic acid − 2H₂O = C₈H₁₄N₂O₂ = the printed –[NH–CH₂–CO–NH–(CH₂)₅–CO]ₙ– unit;
caprolactam C₆H₁₁NO = the printed –[NH–(CH₂)₅–CO]– unit exactly (confirming the card's "no water is
released"), and caprolactam + H₂O = C₆H₁₃NO₂ = 6-aminohexanoic acid (confirming "the cyclic amide
of 6-aminohexanoic acid"); HMDA C₆H₁₆N₂ + adipic acid C₆H₁₀O₄ − 2H₂O = C₁₂H₂₂N₂O₂ = the printed
nylon-6,6 unit; butadiene C₄H₆ − H + Cl = C₄H₅Cl = chloroprene as drawn, CH₂=C(Cl)–CH=CH₂;
styrene C₈H₈ vs the warned-against ethylbenzene C₈H₁₀ differ by H₂ exactly as the mistakes line says;
adipic acid has 4 CH₂ + 2 COOH carbons = 6 C; terephthalic acid HOOC–C₆H₄–COOH = C₈H₆O₄.
Buna-S = butadiene + styrene, Buna-N = butadiene + acrylonitrile, dacron = ethylene glycol +
terephthalic acid, bakelite = phenol + formaldehyde, urea–formaldehyde = urea + HCHO, and the
1,2-acid-gives-glyptal warning are each correct as NCERT states them. The `thermosetting_polymers`
card's correction of the printed book error ("not soft on heating") was re-derived and is itself
right: a thermoset **is** soft when first heated in the mould and only infusible after setting.

## Teacher-gate questions

1. **Nylon-6 called a "condensation" polymer.** `homopolymers` s2 prints "nylon-6 is a condensation
   homopolymer" while `nylon_6_and_nylon_66_monomers` s1 correctly says the ring opens and "no water
   is released". Both follow NCERT, which lists Nylon 6 in its condensation-polymer section even
   though caprolactam polymerisation eliminates no small molecule (it is a ring-opening
   polymerisation). This is a genuine textbook convention, not an error, so it is recorded here
   rather than filed — but a Telangana board teacher should confirm that "condensation" is what the
   marking expects, and whether a student writing "ring-opening polymerisation" is accepted.
2. **Mn / Mw written without the overbar.** The board and the source book print M̄n and M̄w; the bank
   writes `Mn` and `Mw` because Unicode has no subscript "w" (verified: Unicode subscript letters do
   not include w). Confirm the unbarred form is accepted in the written answer.
3. **"Vinyl cyanide" vs "acrylonitrile".** The source book prints vinyl cyanide; NCERT prints
   acrylonitrile. The cards give both names, which should be safe — confirm.
4. **All 12 mark splits are 1 + 1 CLAIMS**, as each card's own verification note says, and there is
   no second-year chemistry board paper in the corpus to back-test against. In particular
   `ziegler_natta_catalyst`'s `insider_note` asserts that the second mark is "high density polythene,
   which the book does not print but the examiner expects" — that claim rests on nothing checkable
   here and needs a teacher to confirm the *use* of the catalyst is examined, not just its
   composition.
