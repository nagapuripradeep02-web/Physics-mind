## Teacher-gate questions

1. **Milk: emulsion or aquasol?** The source book (p.78, SAQ 6) prints milk's type as "Aquasol"; the
   card overrides it to **emulsion** and says so in the `insider_note` and `common_mistakes`. The
   chemistry is not in doubt (both phases liquid ⇒ emulsion), but if a TS examiner marks against the
   book's key a student writing "emulsion" could be penalised. A board teacher should confirm which
   the key accepts — and ideally that both are accepted.
2. **The positive-sol coagulation series.** Does the Telangana key require the NCERT form
   [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ > PO₄³⁻ > SO₄²⁻ > Cl⁻, or is any charge-ordered anion series accepted? This decides
   whether the WEAK row above is a required edit or optional.
3. **Froth flotation as an application of emulsions.** Standard Indian intermediate guides list the
   concentration of ores by froth flotation among the applications of emulsions; NCERT lists it in the
   metallurgy chapter instead. A teacher should rule whether the TS key accepts it, since the card
   currently tells the student it is a mistake.
4. **Enzyme catalysis under "homogeneous".** `catalysis_classification` s3 `common_mistakes[1]` tells
   the student not to give an enzyme reaction as a homogeneous example, on the ground that enzyme
   catalysis "is treated separately" (which is how NCERT organises it). Enzymes in solution with a
   dissolved substrate are, strictly, a single phase, and some texts classify enzyme catalysis as
   homogeneous (others as microheterogeneous, the enzyme being colloidal). Not filed as a finding —
   a board teacher should say whether an enzyme example would be accepted there.
5. **Krafft temperature and solubility.** s2 `common_mistakes[1]` of the CMC card rejects "the
   temperature at which soap dissolves". At NCERT level that is right; in surfactant physical
   chemistry the Krafft point is exactly where surfactant solubility equals the CMC. Left as written
   (NCERT-level answer), flagged only so the teacher knows the card takes a side.
6. All 12 cards carry `needs_teacher_verification: true` and the same standing gap: no two-book union
   check and no board back-test were possible for Chemistry-II (the BLM on hand is physics-only and
   first-year; no second-year chemistry paper is in the corpus). The 1+1 / 1+1+1+1 / 1+2+1 / 2+2 mark
   splits are all the bank's claims.
