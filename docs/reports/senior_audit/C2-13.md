## Teacher-gate questions

1. **Lithium and dinitrogen at room temperature.** `dinitrogen_inert_character` s1 prints the absolute
   "N₂ takes part in **no** reaction at ordinary temperature", and s2 tags 6Li + N₂ → 2Li₃N as
   "(on heating)". That follows NCERT Class 12 Ch.7, which puts the metal nitrides under "at higher
   temperatures". NCERT Class 11 s-block, however, presents lithium as the one alkali metal that
   combines *directly* with dinitrogen, and many Indian texts state it happens at room temperature.
   Should the card soften the absolute, or does the board mark the Class-12 wording?
2. **White phosphorus ignition temperature.** The card says "about 35 °C". Indian textbooks commonly
   quote 30 °C (303 K); CRC gives 34 °C. Which value should the bank print? (The finding above
   rewrites this sentence anyway; the number still needs a ruling.)
3. **The 1+1 mark splits.** Every `verification` note in this unit records the same limitation: the
   split is the bank's own claim, no Telangana second-year chemistry paper is in the corpus, and the
   two-book union check was impossible (the TSBIE Basic Learning Material on hand is physics-only and
   first-year). All nine splits and the `expected_time_min` = 4 convention need a board teacher's
   confirmation. Internally they are consistent — that is all this audit can establish.
4. **"N and P show +5 readily"** (`inert_pair_effect` s2, printed line). The same step's `why` then
   restricts nitrogen's +5 to oxides and oxoacids, since its maximum covalence is 4. Both statements
   are standard, but a teacher should rule whether the bare printed line should carry the qualifier.
