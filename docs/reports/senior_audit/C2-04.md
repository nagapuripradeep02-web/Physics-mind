# C2-04 — Chemistry-II, Unit 2 Solutions (part 2/2)

Cards audited: 12 / 12   Findings: HARMFUL 1 · WRONG 3 · WEAK 8

Every numerical card was solved from `QUESTION:` alone before the steps were read, in a python
script (`numerics.py`): molar masses recomputed atom-by-atom (C₈H₁₈ 114, C₆H₁₂O₆ 180, C₂H₆O₂ 62,
C₂H₄O₂ 60, NaOH 40), every division and rounding re-run, and each dilute-solution answer re-solved
a second time in EXACT Raoult form to measure what the approximation costs (octane 8 g vs 10 g;
glucose 17.4376 vs 17.4381 mm Hg; benzene 170 vs 169.0 g mol⁻¹). `sympy` was used to rearrange the
relative-lowering relation independently and to derive the exact closed form
M₂ = w₂M₁pₛ / (w₁(p⁰ − pₛ)), which is what exposes the two `why` slips below. A second script
(`structure.py`) checked every `mark_split` sum, label pairing and step-marks pairing, and a third
(`prose.py`) swept all 12 cards' `line` / `why` / `common_mistakes` / `memory_tip` / `margin_note` /
`insider_note` strings for register and for verbatim repeats. No card in this group carries a
figure or a `recall` block; the only equation in the group, RCOOH ⇌ RCOO⁻ + H⁺, is atom- and
charge-balanced (0 → −1 + 1).

## HARMFUL — wrong chemistry a student would copy into an exam

| question_id | step_id | what is wrong | what it should say |
|---|---|---|---|
| ts_ipe_c2_sol_relative_lowering_molar_mass | s4_molar_mass | `memory_tip`: "Solute mass and solvent molar mass on top, solvent mass and the lowering below." The boxed formula is M₂ = (w₂ × M₁ × **p⁰**) / (w₁ × (p⁰ − pₛ)) — five quantities — and the tip names only four: **p⁰ is missing from the numerator**. The card itself fixes "the lowering" as (p⁰ − pₛ) (s1 line, and s1 `common_mistakes[0]`: "Without dividing by p⁰ it is the lowering, not the relative lowering"), so the tip cannot be read as meaning the *relative* lowering. A student reciting it writes M₂ = w₂M₁/(w₁(p⁰ − pₛ)), which is dimensionally wrong (g mol⁻¹ bar⁻¹) and, on this unit's own benzene data (w₂ = 0.5, M₁ = 78, w₁ = 39, p⁰ − pₛ = 0.005), gives **200 g mol⁻¹ instead of 170**. Both sibling tips in this group (octane s2, glucose s2) enumerate every factor of their fraction, so the pattern the student is trained on is "the tip lists them all". | "Solute mass, solvent molar mass and p⁰ on top; solvent mass and the lowering (p⁰ − pₛ) below." |

## Teacher-gate questions

1. **`ts_ipe_c2_sol_mass_of_solute_in_octane` — is 8 g or 10 g the marked answer?** The card boxes
   8 g and records the conflict openly. Confirmed independently: the relative lowering here is 0.2,
   so the solute is a fifth of all the moles present and the dilute approximation is not valid.
   Exact Raoult (x₂ = 0.2, n₁ = 114/114 = 1 mol) gives n₂ = 0.25 mol → **10 g**; the approximate
   form the book and NCERT print gives **8 g**. This is a genuine textbook discrepancy, not a bank
   error, and only a Telangana IPE examiner can say which a script is credited for. Note this is
   the only card in the group where the approximation matters: on the glucose card it moves the
   answer by 0.0005 mm Hg and on the benzene card by 1 g mol⁻¹ in 170, both rounding-level.
2. **The four-acid order on `ts_ipe_c2_sol_freezing_point_chloroacetic_acids`.** The question names
   three acids (acetic, dichloroacetic, trichloroacetic) and the book's printed key gives a
   four-term order that also contains monochloroacetic acid. The card writes both. Does the board
   penalise the extra term, or expect only the three asked?
3. **Every mark split in this group is the bank's own claim** — all 12 cards carry
   `verification.status: "unverified"` and `needs_teacher_verification: true`, and the source book
   prints no mark scheme. The four "Problems : In text Questions" cards
   (`prob_mole_fraction_ethylene_glycol`, `prob_molarity_naoh`, `prob_molality_ethanoic_acid`,
   `prob_molar_mass_from_vapour_pressure`) additionally have their **paper section inferred**, not
   read: a book problem belongs to no printed section. The 2/2/2/4-mark placements are reasonable
   but unconfirmed.
4. **`ts_ipe_c2_sol_mole_fraction_definition` `appearances` = [2014, 2015]** is transcribed from a
   printed "(March 2014, June 2015)" citation and has never been checked against a paper — no
   second-year chemistry paper is in the corpus.
