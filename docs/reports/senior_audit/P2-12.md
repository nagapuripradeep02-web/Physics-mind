## Notes

- **`ts_ipe_p2_dnr_heisenberg_uncertainty` — the priority check.** Re-derived independently: the
  statement step ("impossible to measure both position and momentum... to any desired degree of
  accuracy") is the standard textbook wording, and its two `common_mistakes` (not an accuracy limit
  on one quantity alone; not an instrumental limit — it is intrinsic to matter's wave nature) are
  both textbook-correct. The relation step boxes `Δx·Δp ≈ ℏ, ℏ = h/2π`; computed `ℏ/2 = h/4π`
  exactly, confirming the `why`'s cited "standard sharp statement" `Δx·Δp ≥ ℏ/2` is the same
  relation at tighter rigor, not a competing one — and the RECALL `accept` list on the relation step
  already credits both the `≈ ℏ` form and the `≥ ℏ/2` form, so no correct student answer is
  rejected. No printed line, boxed answer, mistake, margin note or recall field is false or
  self-contradictory. Verdict: **the independent grading pass that flagged this card was mistaken.**
  The one debatable point — stating an approximate and an exact form "say the same thing" — is a
  legitimate pedagogical simplification the source book itself makes (see Teacher-gate above), not
  an error, so it is not filed as a finding.
- **Cross-card physics consistency confirmed by algebra**, not just per-card reading: Einstein's
  `E = W₀ + ½mv_max²` (equation card) combines with `eV₀ = ½mv_max²` (definition of stopping
  potential, intensity/potential card) to give exactly `eV₀ = h(ν − ν₀)` (the boxed result on the
  frequency/stopping-potential card) — all three cards agree with each other and with the standard
  derivation.
