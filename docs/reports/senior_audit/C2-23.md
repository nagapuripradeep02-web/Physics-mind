## HARMFUL — wrong chemistry a student would copy into an exam

| question_id | step_id | what is wrong | what it should say |
|---|---|---|---|
| ts_ipe_c2_bio_saq_starch_vs_cellulose | s1_common | `lines` + `common_mistakes[1]`. The card states the C₁–C₄ linkage as universal for BOTH polymers: "In both, one unit is joined to the next by a glycosidic linkage between C₁ of one and C₄ of the next", and `common_mistakes[1]` hardens it into a rule — "Saying they are joined at different carbon atoms. **Both use the C₁ to C₄ linkage.**" That is false for starch. Amylopectin — which the card's own s2_starch names as one of starch's two components — is branched, and NCERT is explicit that in amylopectin "the chain is formed by C₁–C₄ glycosidic linkage whereas **branching occurs by C₁–C₆ glycosidic linkage**." A branch is geometrically impossible at C₄, so the card asserts something that cannot be true of the polymer it has just called branched. Worse, `common_mistakes[1]` would tell a student who correctly writes the C₁–C₆ branch linkage that they have made a mistake. s2_starch compounds it: "In both parts the units are joined by α-glycosidic linkages" never names C₁–C₆ either, so nothing in the card supplies it. A student who carries "starch: α, C₁–C₄ only" into any structure-of-starch question loses the branch-linkage mark. | s1_common lines: "In both, the **main chain** is joined by a glycosidic linkage between C₁ of one unit and C₄ of the next." `common_mistakes[1]`: "Saying the two main chains are joined at different carbon atoms. Both main chains use the C₁ to C₄ linkage; only amylopectin has C₁–C₆ linkages, and those are its branch points." And add one line to s2_starch: "In amylopectin the branches are joined by α-C₁–C₆ glycosidic linkages." |

## Teacher-gate questions

1. **Essential amino acid count.** The card follows the board key's 10 essential / 10 non-essential
   split (which is also NCERT's — ten entries are starred in its amino-acid table). Modern nutrition
   lists nine strictly essential for an adult, with histidine essential in children. The card's
   `verification` note already records the divergence and keeps the board's 10/10 on the page. A
   Telangana IPE teacher should confirm 10/10 is still what the key marks.
2. **"Cyclic hemiacetal" for a ketose.** s1 of `ts_ipe_c2_bio_anomers` defines anomers as "the two
   cyclic **hemiacetal** forms" and then extends the definition to C₂ of a ketose, whose cyclic form
   is strictly a hemiketal. IUPAC now treats hemiketal as a subclass of hemiacetal, so the card is
   defensible and matches NCERT's register — but confirm the board does not want "hemiketal" for
   fructose. Not filed as a finding.
3. **HI vs HI + red phosphorus** (see the WRONG row). Does the board's key accept the bare "HI" that
   NCERT and the question stem both print?
4. **Amylopectin's C₁–C₆ branch linkage** (see the HARMFUL row). Confirm the key expects it named in
   a starch-vs-cellulose comparison — the fix above adds it, and it costs one line.
5. **Invert-sugar rotation −20°.** The source book prints no value; −20° is derived here from
   +52.5° (glucose) and −92.4° (fructose) and matches the standard texts. Confirm the key accepts a
   numeric value rather than "laevorotatory" alone.
6. **"Amla"** is kept as printed in `ts_ipe_c2_bio_vitamin_a_and_c_sources` (the answer key names it).
   Correct for this board; flagged only because it is the one region-specific word in the group.
7. **"Saccharic acid" / "glucaric acid".** The card's own margin note says either is accepted;
   confirm against the key.

## Notes

- **The chemistry is otherwise sound.** Every equation balances, every condensed structure resolves
  to the right molecular formula, every named product is the right one (n-hexane / gluconic acid /
  saccharic acid; glucose + fructose from sucrose), every reagent-strength claim is right
  (Br₂ water mild → C1 only; conc. HNO₃ strong → C1 and C6), and every standard value matches NCERT:
  sucrose +66.5°, glucose +52.5°, fructose −92.4°, invert mixture ≈ −20°, starch hydrolysis at
  **dilute H₂SO₄, 393 K, 2–3 atm**. Sucrose's C₁-of-glucose-to-C₂-of-fructose linkage and the
  both-anomeric-carbons reason for its being non-reducing are correct and well argued.
- **Two book errors are correctly caught and corrected by the bank**, and I re-derived both
  corrections: "hemiacetyl" → **hemiacetal** (`anomers`), and the printed "H₅N⁺" → **H₃N⁺**
  (`zwitter_ion_vsaq`) — nitrogen carries three hydrogens in the zwitterion, and the atom/charge
  balance confirms it (C2 H5 N1 O2, net 0, both sides). The book's "building blocks of proteins"
  figure of speech was also correctly replaced with literal wording in `amino_acids_definition`.
- **Structure is perfect across the group.** 13/13 cards: step marks sum to `marks_total`,
  `mark_split` sums to `marks_total`, and every `mark_split` label and mark matches its step exactly
  in order. `expected_time_min` is 4 for every 2-mark VSAQ and 8 for every 4-mark SAQ — no drift.
  Exactly one `boxed_final` step per card, always last.
- **No `recall` block anywhere in the group** (0/13 cards, 0/32 steps), so there is no grader-side
  rubric here that could reject a correct student answer. **No figures** in the group either — check
  5 of the brief has nothing to report.
- **No duplicated prose.** All 32 steps' `why`, `memory_tip`, `margin_note` and every
  `common_mistakes` entry are distinct strings across the whole group; nothing is cloned from a
  sibling card.
- **`common_mistakes` were checked one by one for the "condemning a correct answer" defect.** Only
  the red-phosphorus item (filed WRONG) is one. Two others are completeness advice rather than error

  `common_mistakes[1]` ("Leaving out the word α") — the general two-group definition is correct, and
