## HARMFUL — wrong content a student would copy into an exam

| question_id | step_id | what is wrong | what it should say |

| ts_ipe_m2b_cir_point_of_contact_x_minus2y_minus5 | card (`insider_note`) | "The formula has a minus sign in front and n in the denominator, so a negative n turns both coordinates positive again — writing the signs one at a time is safer than doing it in the head." This is false for THIS card: n = −5 is negative, yet the card's own boxed point of contact is (1, −2) — x is positive but y is **negative**. Verified: x = −(1)(5)/(−5) = 1, y = −(−2)(5)/(−5) = −2. A negative n does not turn "both coordinates positive"; each coordinate takes the sign that results from combining n's sign with l's or m's sign separately, and here l and m have opposite signs, so x and y come out with opposite signs. A student told to expect "both positive" and getting y = −2 would doubt a correct answer, or "fix" it to (1, 2) — which the card's own MISTAKE[1] ("Reporting the answer as (−1, 2)") shows is exactly the kind of sign slip this note should be warning against, not encouraging. | "The formula has a minus sign in front and n in the denominator — trace the sign of each coordinate separately from l, m and n; do not assume both come out the same sign. Here n = −5 with l = 1 gives x = +1, but n = −5 with m = −2 gives y = −2, not positive." |
