## Teacher-gate questions

1. **`ts_ipe_c2_sol_molarity_10_percent_glucose` — which value does the board mark?** Three answers
   are in circulation for the same printed question and they are not reconcilable: the source book
   prints **0.617 M** (arithmetically the *molality*, 0.617 mol kg⁻¹ — reproduced exactly by its own
   route 10/180 × 1000/90, and wrong as a molarity); this card boxes **0.56 M** on a stated
   assumption of d = 1.00 g mL⁻¹; NCERT's version of the problem supplies d = 1.2 g mL⁻¹ and answers
   **0.67 M**. For information, the real density of 10% w/w aqueous glucose is ≈ 1.04 g mL⁻¹, which
   gives ≈ 0.58 M — so the card's correction is the closest of the three to the true value and the
   book's is definitely not a molarity. But if a TS IPE examiner marks against the book's key, a
   student who writes 0.56 M loses the mark. **A board teacher must rule** whether the card should
   keep 0.56 M, switch to the NCERT 0.67 M with a stated density, or present the book's 0.617 M with
   the caveat. Everything downstream of this (the s2 `why`, both `common_mistakes`, the
   `insider_note`) hangs on the ruling.
2. **Kb / Kf definitions and the word "non-volatile".** Both constant cards require the solute to be
   non-volatile in the *definition* of the constant. That matches NCERT's framing (both colligative
   effects are introduced for a non-volatile solute) and every Indian text, but for freezing-point
   depression the physically necessary condition is that the solute does not enter the solid phase,
   not that it is non-volatile. Kept as written; a teacher should confirm the board accepts (and
   expects) "non-volatile" in the Kf definition.
3. **Mark split is the bank's claim.** Every card in this group is cut 1 + 1 with
   `expected_time_min` 4, and every `VERIFICATION_NOTE` says so. Unconfirmed by any board document —
   audited here only for internal consistency, per the brief.
