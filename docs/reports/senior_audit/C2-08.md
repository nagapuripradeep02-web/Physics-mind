## Teacher-gate questions

1. **The 0-mark diagram claim — the one item a board teacher must settle.** Two cards state that the
   energy-profile diagram earns nothing. `ts_ipe_c2_ck_activation_energy` is asked as "Explain the term
   activation energy of a reaction **with a suitable diagram**" and its `insider_note` (spoken first to the
   student) asserts "Two marks sit entirely on the words, **because the question does not say draw**";
   `ts_ipe_c2_ck_catalyst_effect_on_rate` is asked "Discuss the effect of catalyst … **with diagram**" and its
   s5 `margin_note` repeats the reasoning. Both questions do ask for a diagram; the card's distinction rests
   only on the absence of the literal word "draw". The advice that follows ("Draw it only if the endothermic
   one is already finished and there is time"; "sketch it only after they are written") is exam-costing if a
   TS examiner does allocate a mark to the profile. The mark splits are internally consistent either way
   (2 = 1+1 and 8 = 2+2+2+2 with the diagram steps at 0), so this cannot be decided from the bank.
2. **"Activated collision" as a synonym for "effective/fruitful collision"** (WRONG row 2 above). Several
   Indian texts print the three as synonyms, and the source book appears to. Does TS marking accept a
   definition of an effective collision that mentions energy only, or must orientation be in it?
3. **2NO(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO₂(g) as a genuine trimolecular elementary step.** NCERT prints it as the standard
   trimolecular example and the card relies on reading the molecularity straight off it; modern kinetics
   prefers a two-step route through N₂O₂. Keep as-is for the board, but a teacher should confirm.
4. **Every card carries an `insider_note` asserting examiner behaviour, while `appearances` is empty on all
   nine and the verification notes record that no second-year chemistry board paper is in the corpus and no
   back-test was possible.** Claims such as "The brackets round a/(a − x) are worth a mark on their own",
   "This question is marked on the signs, not on the algebra" and "The orientation condition and the steric
   factor P carry two of the eight marks" have no evidence behind them beyond the bank's own mark-split
   claim. They are pedagogically sound but should be read by a teacher before they are spoken to students.
5. **The hedged temperature coefficient.** Both temperature cards deliberately soften the book's flat
   statement to "As a rough rule … not an exact law". Chemically this is right (2–3 corresponds to
   Eₐ ≈ 54–85 kJ mol⁻¹ near 300 K, which I recomputed), but a teacher should confirm the board does not
   penalise the qualifier.
