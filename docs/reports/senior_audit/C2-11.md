## Teacher-gate questions

1. **German silver composition (the HARMFUL row above).** Three candidates are in play: the FAST TRACK book's Cu 25–40 / Zn 25–35 / Ni 40–50 (impossible as printed), the commonly printed Indian-textbook Cu 25–30 / Zn 25–30 / Ni 40–50 (still Ni-major, bounds sum 90–110), and the real alloy Cu 60 / Zn 20 / Ni 20. A Telangana IPE teacher must say which the board marks. Until then the card should not print a set of ranges that cannot sum to 100 %.
2. **Brass.** The card prints the book's single pair Cu 60 % / Zn 40 %; most textbooks print the range Cu 60–80 % / Zn 20–40 %. Does the board accept either?
3. **Bauxite formula.** The card writes Al₂O₃·2H₂O (Indian intermediate convention, also what the book prints); NCERT Table 6.1 writes AlOₓ(OH)₃₋₂ₓ. Confirm the board marks Al₂O₃·2H₂O. Not filed as an error — it is a genuine convention difference.
4. **Copper smelting vessel.** Both this group (`matte`) and the sibling `copper_from_copper_pyrites` say roasting in a reverberatory furnace and smelting in a **blast furnace**, which is the classic Indian description; some texts smelt in the reverberatory furnace. Internally consistent across the unit — confirm it matches the Telangana syllabus.
5. **983 K.** Standard thermodynamic data give the C/CO ↔ CO/CO₂ crossover at 980.9 K; the book (and both cards) say 983 K. Confirm 983 K is the figure the board expects, since the cards make it the memorised number.
6. **The 1 + 1 mark split** on all nine cards is the bank's own claim, as every `verification` note states. Nothing in the split is internally inconsistent, but none of it is board-sourced.

## Notes

- **All chemistry except the German silver composition is correct.** Every equation printed in the
  group balances: ZnO + C → Zn + CO; 2C + O₂ → 2CO; 2CO + O₂ → 2CO₂; 2FeS + 3O₂ → 2FeO + 2SO₂;
  FeO + SiO₂ → FeSiO₃. All three equations the cards condemn as student errors are genuinely
  unbalanced (ZnO + C → Zn + CO₂ has O 1≠2; 2CO + O₂ → CO₂ has C 2≠1; FeS + SiO₂ → FeSiO₃ + S has
  O 2≠3), so those `common_mistakes` entries are real. Every formula and ore name checks out against
  NCERT Table 6.1: bauxite, cryolite Na₃AlF₆, zincite ZnO, zinc blende ZnS, haematite Fe₂O₃,
  magnetite Fe₃O₄, cuprite Cu₂O, copper glance Cu₂S, copper pyrites CuFeS₂.
- **The Ellingham pair is the strongest work in the group.** The crossover recomputed independently
  from ΔH°f/S° lands at 980.9 K against the book's 983 K; the sign of the slope on each line is
  explained correctly (1 mol gas → 2 mol gas falls; 3 mol gas → 2 mol gas rises); 983 K → 710 °C is
  exact; and the direction of the comparison is right on both sides of the crossing. The C line at
  1673 K sits at −520 kJ against −220 kJ for the zinc line, so "carbon reduces ZnO at 1673 K" is
  confirmed, and the two cross near 1188 K, well below the furnace temperature.
- **Book errors the cards corrected — all re-derived and all correct.** "Zincate → ZnO" corrected to
  **zincite** (a zincate is the [Zn(OH)₄]²⁻ ion formed in alkali, charge verified, not a mineral);
  the flux definition corrected from "added to the ore to lower its m.p." to reacting with the
  infusible gangue to give a fusible slag; "Heamatite" → haematite; P₄O₁₀ chosen over P₂O₅ (same
  oxide, molecular vs empirical formula, chemistry unaffected). The card that reproduced a book
  problem rather than correcting it — German silver — is the one that produced the HARMFUL row.

  label matches its step's `label` exactly; every `mark_note` ("Reason", "Effects", "Two alloys",
  "Definition", "Examples", "Answer", "Role", "Equations", "Composition", "Al and Zn", "Fe and Cu",
  "Gangue", "Slag") describes work the step actually does; `expected_time_min` = 4 on all nine, and

- **Nothing to audit under checks 5 and half of 3.** No card in this group has a `figure`, a `recall`
