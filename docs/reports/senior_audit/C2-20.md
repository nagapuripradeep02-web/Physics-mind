## Teacher-gate questions

- **Semi-synthetic polymers — is vulcanised rubber accepted?** `ts_ipe_c2_pol_semi_synthetic_polymers`
  `insider_note` says "Both must be cellulose derivatives". NCERT gives cellulose acetate and
  cellulose nitrate as "the usual examples", but several Indian texts also list vulcanised rubber
  under semi-synthetic. If a TS IPE examiner accepts vulcanised rubber, the insider note
  over-restricts a correct answer and should read "the safe pair is the two cellulose derivatives".
- **30 % sulphur — "ebonite" or "battery case rubber"?** Both vulcanisation cards say ~30 % sulphur
  "gives the hard rubber called ebonite". NCERT's own sentence names *battery case rubber*. The two
  are the same material and the chemistry is right either way, but a strict key-matching examiner
  may want the NCERT noun.
- **PDI = 1 for natural polymers.** The card states it flatly ("Natural polymers have chains of one
  length"); NCERT hedges ("generally monodispersed"), and outside the syllabus cellulose, starch and
  natural rubber are all polydisperse. The card's `common_mistakes[1]` ("Writing PDI > 1 for natural
  polymers") would mark a student wrong who knows the wider fact. Confirm the board wants the flat
  NCERT statement.
- **The question stem `–[NH₂–CHR–CO]ₙ–`** on `ts_ipe_c2_pol_polyamide_homopolymer_or_copolymer` is
  reproduced from the source book and is chemically impossible (nitrogen gets four bonds — machine
  checked). The bank deliberately keeps it and spends mark 2 correcting it to `–[NH–CHR–CO]ₙ–`,
  which is how NCERT prints the same question. Confirm the board reprints the book's NH₂ form; if it
  reprints NCERT's NH form, the stem should be changed and mark 2 re-cut.
