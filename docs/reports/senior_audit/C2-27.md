## HARMFUL — wrong chemistry a student would copy into an exam

| ts_ipe_c2_hal_conversions_ethane_and_toluene | card | `insider_note`: "The last step is aqueous KOH, not alcoholic — **alcoholic KOH would give an alkene instead of the alcohol**." False for this substrate. The substrate at that step is benzyl chloride, C₆H₅–CH₂–Cl. Its only neighbouring carbon is an aromatic ring carbon that carries no available hydrogen, and a C=C into the ring would destroy the aromatic sextet, so benzyl chloride has **no β-hydrogen and cannot eliminate at all**. With alcoholic KOH it still substitutes, giving benzyl ethyl ether (the ethoxide ion competing with hydroxide), never an alkene. The advice "use aqueous KOH" is right; the reason printed under it is wrong, and it is the first thing the chatbot says to the student. | "The last step is aqueous KOH: it replaces –Cl by –OH and gives benzyl alcohol. Alcoholic KOH is the reagent for elimination, but benzyl chloride has no β-hydrogen, so it cannot give an alkene — with alcoholic KOH the ethoxide ion competes and gives benzyl ethyl ether instead." |
| ts_ipe_c2_hal_conversions_ethane_and_toluene | s2_toluene_to_benzyl_alcohol | `common_mistakes[1]`: "Using alcoholic KOH in step 2. **Alcoholic KOH removes HCl and gives an alkene**; aqueous KOH gives the alcohol." Same false claim as the insider note, in the field a student reads while writing the step. A student who repeats this reason in an exam, or who applies it to "which of these gives an alkene with alcoholic KOH", writes false chemistry. | "Using alcoholic KOH in step 2.
