Every card was answered from the `QUESTION:` line first, against NCERT Class 12 Chapter 1 (Solid
State), before the printed lines were read. The two counting cards were machine-checked with a
python script (`8 + 6 = 14`, `8·⅛ + 6·½ = 4`; `8 + 1 = 9`, `8·⅛ + 1·1 = 2`, exact fractions) and

figures were re-plotted from their stroke coordinates: the checkerboard parity of every drawn ion,
which sub-lattice each vacancy box sits on, the interstitial position, and every leader endpoint.
There are no chemical equations in this group to atom- or charge-balance; the redox/oxidation-state

key-scan of all ten JSONs: step keys are `lines / why / common_mistakes / memory_tip / margin_note /

## Teacher-gate questions

   "The question says lattice points, so 14 is the answer being marked" / "…so 9…". That is NCERT's
   own exercise answer and the card safely prints both numbers with working, but Indian classroom
   practice on this question splits, and the insider note is spoken to the student first. A
   Telangana IPE chemistry teacher should confirm which number the examiner boxes.
2. **Is "glass" in this board question intended as quartz glass?** NCERT's comparison is quartz vs
   quartz glass. If the board expects plain "glass", the WRONG row above still stands as the
   chemically safe wording; if the board's own model answer says "glass is SiO₂", record that.
3. **Frenkel-defect synonyms:** does the board accept "interstitial defect" as a name for the whole
   Frenkel defect, or only "dislocation defect"? (WEAK row above.)

   Nothing in this audit can confirm or refute the split — only its internal consistency, which is
   clean apart from the one label mismatch above.

## Notes

- **Every boxed answer in the group is chemically correct.** The re-derivations that matter:
  amorphous = irregular arrangement, short range order only, isotropic, softens over a range
  (glass/rubber/plastic); glass amorphous vs quartz crystalline; hcp = ABAB, ccp = ABCABC, third
  layer over the first vs into new voids, both 74 % and CN 12, hcp Mg/Zn, ccp Cu/Ag, ccp ≡ fcc;
  crystal lattice = the whole arrangement, unit cell = smallest repeating portion fixed by a, b, c,
  α, β, γ; fcc 14 points / 4 particles and bcc 9 points / 2 particles (both machine-checked);
  Schottky = equal numbers of cations and anions absent, density falls, NaCl/KCl/CsCl/AgBr;
  Frenkel = smaller cation to an interstitial site, density unchanged, AgCl/AgBr/ZnS; F-centre =
  anion vacancy holding an unpaired electron from heating in the metal vapour, a metal-excess
  defect, NaCl yellow / LiCl pink / KCl violet; ferromagnetism = strong attraction, domains,
  permanent magnetism, Fe/Co/Ni/Gd/CrO₂, CrO₂ on recording tape, paramagnetic above the Curie
  temperature. All ten agree with NCERT Class 12 Chapter 1.
- **The AgBr double-listing is handled correctly, not a contradiction.** AgBr appears in both the
  Schottky and Frenkel example lists, and both cards say so explicitly in their `why` ("it is one of
  the few solids that shows both defects"). That is right, and the cross-card consistency is
  deliberate — I checked it before reading the explanations.
- **The Schottky figure is fully correct.** Re-plotted from coordinates: the 3 × 3 grid is a valid
  checkerboard (row 0 `+ − +`, row 1 `− + −`, row 2 `+ − +`), the empty row-0 middle site is an
  anion position and carries the "anion vacancy" label, the empty row-1 middle site is a cation
  position and carries the "cation vacancy" label, and both leader arrowheads land on their boxes.
  The Frenkel figure's parity, vacancy sub-lattice (cation, correct), interstitial ion at the exact
  gap midpoint (152, 96) and move-arrow direction (vacancy → interstitial) are all correct too; only
  the one leader endpoint is loose (WEAK above).
- **Both zero-mark diagram steps are internally consistent**: `marks = 0`, no `mark_split` row, no
  `mark_note`, running total held at 2 → 2, and both `margin_note`s tell the student the diagram
  earns nothing because the question does not say "draw".
- **`insider_note`: 10 / 10 present, and 10 / 10 check out.** Each names a real marking point
  (amorphous/crystalline in the same answer; hcp = ABAB not swapped; "smallest" + "repeated in three
  dimensions"; vacancy + equal numbers; density unchanged; anion vacancy not just vacancy; "strongly"
  + "permanent"). The only ones needing outside confirmation are the two lattice-point notes, raised
  under Teacher-gate.
- **Rule 41: clean.** No idioms, no metaphors, no personification anywhere in the ten cards' 60-odd
  reader-facing strings. "Coin metals" (coinage metals), "building block", "stacking" and "trapped
  electron" are chemistry register, not figurative language. Nothing filed.
- **Duplicated prose is twin-card duplication only** and reads as intended: the fcc/bcc pair share a
  `margin_note` ("Name the two sets of positions before the arithmetic…") and an `insider_note`
  shape, and the Schottky/Frenkel pair share the diagram `margin_note`. Both pairs are deliberate
  parallel construction, and each instance is true of its own card — not filed.
- **`expected_time_min` = 4 on all ten**, `marks_total` = 2 on all ten, `qtype` = VSAQ,
  `paper_section` = "Section A", `page_header` identical across the group. Consistent.
- **Crystallographic aside, not a finding:** a *face-centred tetragonal* lattice is not one of the 14
  Bravais lattices — it reduces to a smaller body-centred tetragonal cell. NCERT nevertheless sets
  the question and its answer is 14, which is what the card prints, so the card is right for the
  exam. Worth knowing if a student asks.
- **NaCl "cation and anion of nearly the same size"** (Schottky s2, and the mirror line in Frenkel
  s2 `common_mistakes[1]`) is NCERT's own wording for the Schottky condition, so it is kept even
  though Na⁺ (95 pm) and Cl⁻ (181 pm) are not close on a radius-ratio view. Not filed — it matches
  the syllabus text a student is marked against.
