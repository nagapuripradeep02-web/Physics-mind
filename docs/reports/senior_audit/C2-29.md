## Teacher-gate questions

1. **Dow-process pressure — 320 atm or 300 atm?** NCERT Class 12 prints 623 K / **320 atm**; the source book prints 320 atm here and 300 atm at its own Very Short Answer 5, and Indian problem banks circulate both. The HARMFUL row above assumes the Telangana key follows NCERT. A board teacher must confirm which figure the marking scheme accepts, and whether the other is accepted as an alternative (in which case the mistakes entry should be deleted, not inverted).
2. **Does phenol react with Na₂CO₃?** `ts_ipe_c2_ape_kolbe_reimer_tiemann_williamson` s1_kolbe_phenoxide `MISTAKE[1]` says "Using Na₂CO₃ instead of NaOH. Phenol is too weak an acid to be converted by a carbonate." That is the standard school-textbook line and I have NOT filed it as a finding, but the numbers cut the other way: K = Ka(phenol)/Ka₂(H₂CO₃) = 10⁻⁹·⁹⁸/10⁻¹⁰·³³ ≈ **2.2**, so carbonate does deprotonate phenol appreciably (no CO₂ is evolved, which is the observation the textbooks are really describing). With bicarbonate the same calculation gives K ≈ 2×10⁻⁴, i.e. genuinely no reaction. A board teacher should rule whether "phenol does not react with sodium carbonate" is the wording the key expects.
3. **NaOH test wording.** Related to the second WRONG row: does the board's key state the phenol/alcohol test as "phenol dissolves in NaOH, an alcohol does not" (the traditional qualitative-analysis phrasing, true only for water-insoluble alcohols) or as "phenol reacts with NaOH, an alcohol does not" (true for every alcohol, and what NCERT says)? Both appear in Indian texts.
4. **Reimer-Tiemann equation form.** The card prints the fully balanced overall change (C₆H₅OH + CHCl₃ + 3NaOH → salicylaldehyde + 3NaCl + 2H₂O, which I verified balances and equals the stage-sum after cancelling NaOH against HCl); the source book prints reagents over an arrow with no NaCl or H₂O at all. Confirm the balanced form is accepted rather than penalised as "not the book's scheme".
5. **`ts_ipe_c2_ape_sn2_reactivity_pairs` is a haloalkanes question** printed inside this unit's sub-box (both pairs are haloalkanes; neither is an alcohol, phenol or ether — the `verification_note` records this). Confirm it stays under Unit 16 for the student's revision, since a student revising alcohols/phenols/ethers will not expect an SN2 question here.
